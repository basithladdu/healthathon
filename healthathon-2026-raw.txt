Guide
Assistive, not diagnostic. Keep this in mind at every stage.

What is Health-a-thon 2026?
India’s first Doctor × Technology health hackathon. Doctors name real operational problems; technologists help design and build assistive AI solutions - not diagnostic tools - for Cancer Care, Diabetes Care, and Maternal & Child Health.

Who can join
Practising doctors, medical students (PG / final year / interns), NCG · RSSDI · FOGSI network members, startups, developers, AI builders, researchers, and final-year engineering students. Register solo or as a team of 2–5 with one clinical lead (practising doctor) and one technical lead.

The hard rule
Assistive, not diagnostic. Solutions may simplify workflows (follow-ups, documentation, navigation, coordination). They must not diagnose, prescribe, replace clinical judgement, or act as clinical decision support. Use only fake or fully anonymised data.

How it works
Doctors and healthcare professionals register and select a challenge → technology teams join from 15 August → you pick your team → Round 1 idea submission → shortlist → build sprint with mentors → Top 30 announced by 14 November → grand finale at IIT Bombay on 28 November 2026.

Key registration windows
Doctor & healthcare professional registration: now – 21 August 2026. Technology team registration: 15 August – 11 September 2026. Matchmaking & team formation: 22 August – 18 September. Round 1 idea submission: 12 – 25 September. Round 1 evaluation & shortlisting: 26 September – 3 October. Build sprint: 5 October – 8 November. Top teams announced by 14 November. Grand finale: 28 November 2026 at IIT Bombay, Powai.

Prizes
₹54+ Lakhs prize pool, split evenly across the three clinical tracks. Top 30 teams reach the finale; every finalist is awarded. Cash prizes, technology credits and partner benefits apply.

Guardrail: Out of Scope: Clinical AI solutions involving diagnosis, treatment recommendations, clinical decision support, clinical risk scoring, interpretation of medical data or autonomous clinical advice.

Patient Follow-up & Continuity of Care
Keep patients connected across months of care - surface who needs follow-up before they are lost to care.
The current reality
Long-term conditions such as cancer, diabetes and maternal health require patients to remain engaged with care over months or years.
Many patients miss follow-up appointments, investigations, treatment milestones or scheduled reviews during their care journey.
Hospitals invest significant effort in identifying and re-engaging these patients to ensure continuity of care.
Relevant information is often fragmented across appointment systems, EMRs, laboratory records, treatment registers, spreadsheets and manual tracking processes.
Doctors and care teams spend considerable time identifying patients who require follow-up, prioritising interventions and coordinating activities across multiple departments.
As patient volumes increase, these manual processes become increasingly difficult to sustain, resulting in delayed interventions and gaps in continuity of care.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that help healthcare teams ensure patients remain connected throughout their care journey - enabling early identification of patients requiring follow-up and timely interventions before they are lost to care.
Improve visibility across fragmented patient information to support informed decision-making, facilitate coordinated follow-up across doctors and care teams, and simplify follow-up workflows within existing healthcare settings.
Reduce avoidable gaps in care by strengthening continuity of care and patient engagement, enabling healthcare providers to deliver timely, coordinated and patient-centred follow-up at scale.

Use case 02 of 07

Consultation Readiness & Patient Journey Review
Start every consult with a complete, longitudinal view of the patient - not a scramble across systems.
The current reality
Preparing for a follow-up consultation involves much more than reviewing a patient's medical record. Doctors and care teams often need to reconstruct the patient's clinical journey from consultation notes, laboratory investigations, imaging reports, treatment records, medication history and pending clinical actions.
In long-term care settings such as cancer, diabetes and maternal health, every consultation builds upon previous decisions. Clinicians need to quickly understand what has changed since the last visit, what remains pending and which issues require immediate attention.
Much of this information is fragmented across multiple systems or exists only as scanned reports, PDFs, images and patient-provided documents, making it difficult to obtain a complete picture before the consultation.
As outpatient clinics become busier, clinicians have limited time to assemble this information, resulting in increased administrative effort and less time for meaningful patient interaction.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that help doctors and care teams begin every consultation with a complete, contextual and longitudinal understanding of the patient's care journey by bringing together clinically relevant information from diverse healthcare data sources.
Enable clinicians to quickly identify meaningful clinical changes, pending investigations, care milestones, medication history and other information required to support informed decision-making during the consultation.
Support the interpretation of structured and unstructured information - including EMRs, laboratory systems, imaging reports, prescriptions, discharge summaries, referral letters, scanned documents, PDFs, photographs and patient-provided records - while identifying missing or inconsistent information and enabling clinicians to gather additional context where required.

Use case 03 of 07

Patient Registry & Population Health
A live view of the whole panel - care gaps, priorities and longitudinal journeys at population scale.
The current reality
Doctors and care teams managing patients with chronic and long-term conditions such as cancer, diabetes and maternal health need to continuously monitor patient populations across different stages of their care journey - not just during clinic visits, but throughout the entire continuum of care.
They need to identify patients requiring attention, recognise emerging care gaps, prioritise outreach and ensure that investigations, treatments, screenings and follow-up milestones are completed on time.
Information required to manage these patient populations is often fragmented across EMRs, disease registries, laboratory systems, spreadsheets and programme databases, making it difficult to maintain a comprehensive and up-to-date view of patients under their care.
As patient volumes increase, manually tracking longitudinal care journeys and coordinating timely interventions becomes increasingly challenging, limiting the ability to deliver proactive, continuous and population-based care.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that enable doctors and care teams to maintain a comprehensive, longitudinal and real-time view of the patient populations under their care, supporting proactive population health management.
Consolidate information from multiple healthcare systems to generate meaningful patient- and population-level insights, identify emerging care gaps, highlight patients requiring attention and support informed clinical and operational decision-making.
Enable coordinated, proactive and continuous care by making it easier to monitor longitudinal care journeys, manage disease registries and support timely interventions across the continuum of care.

Use case 04 of 07

Clinic Operations & Patient Flow
Run busy OPDs with real-time flow, smarter slots and fewer bottlenecks - without disrupting care.
The current reality
Delivering timely and coordinated care in busy outpatient clinics requires continuous coordination between doctors, nurses, front-desk teams and care coordinators while managing appointments, patient arrivals, no-shows, walk-ins, delayed investigations, last-minute cancellations and changing clinical priorities.
As patient volumes increase, balancing clinic capacity with patient demand becomes increasingly challenging, requiring continuous adjustments to schedules, patient flow and resource allocation throughout the day.
Care teams often rely on multiple systems, manual coordination and real-time decision-making to accommodate urgent cases, minimise delays, and ensure patients receive the right care at the right time.
Inefficient patient flow can result in longer waiting times, underutilised clinic capacity, operational bottlenecks and missed opportunities to deliver timely care.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that help clinics proactively manage appointments, patient flow and day-to-day operations while supporting doctors, care teams and administrative staff in responding to real-time operational changes.
Optimise clinic operations by supporting intelligent appointment management, dynamic scheduling, patient queue management, utilisation of cancelled or vacant appointment slots, prioritisation based on clinical urgency and coordinated communication with patients and care teams.
Provide real-time visibility into clinic operations, proactively identify operational bottlenecks and enable timely recommendations that improve clinic efficiency, patient experience and care delivery without disrupting existing workflows.

Use case 05 of 07

Doctor Productivity & Knowledge Assistant
An assistive knowledge and content co-pilot for teaching, research and admin - clinician stays in control.
The current reality
Doctors spend a significant amount of time on professional activities beyond direct patient care, including creating educational material, presentations, research manuscripts, referral communications, patient information, departmental reports, clinical protocols and other professional content.
They also invest considerable effort in reviewing medical literature, clinical guidelines, research publications and other sources of information to support clinical practice, teaching, research and academic work.
These activities are often repetitive, context-dependent and require information to be gathered, synthesised, adapted and presented for different purposes and audiences.
As clinical, academic and administrative responsibilities continue to grow, managing this workload becomes increasingly challenging, reducing the time available for patient care, research, education and innovation.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that act as an intelligent productivity and knowledge assistant for doctors, supporting professional, academic and administrative activities while ensuring clinicians remain in complete control of the final output.
Assist clinicians in creating, reviewing, refining, summarising, adapting and organising professional content by leveraging available clinical context, institutional knowledge, medical literature and user preferences.
Support knowledge discovery, evidence synthesis, content creation, educational material development, research activities and other routine tasks that improve productivity while reducing repetitive cognitive and administrative effort.

Use case 06 of 07

Patient Education & Digital Engagement
Create accurate, multilingual education and campaigns without turning clinicians into full-time content teams.
The current reality
Patient education and engagement are essential for the long-term management of conditions such as cancer, diabetes and maternal and child health. Beyond consultations, doctors and care teams need to continuously educate patients on disease awareness, lifestyle modifications, medication adherence, preventive care, treatment milestones and self-management, while addressing misinformation and improving health literacy.
Sustaining meaningful patient engagement requires significant time and effort. Clinicians are increasingly expected to create educational material, patient handouts, awareness campaigns, social media content, videos, presentations, newsletters, blogs, patient stories and multilingual communication tailored to different audiences and stages of the care journey.
Planning communication calendars, adapting content across multiple digital channels, maintaining clinical accuracy and delivering consistent outreach can become a significant operational burden, often competing with direct patient care and other professional responsibilities.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that enable doctors and care teams to efficiently create, manage and deliver accurate, engaging and context-aware patient education and communication across multiple digital and offline channels.
Support the creation and adaptation of educational content, presentation material, newsletters, blogs, video scripts, awareness campaigns, multilingual communication and personalised educational journeys tailored to different patient populations and stages of care.
Simplify content planning, communication calendars, campaign management and ongoing patient engagement while ensuring clinicians remain in complete control of the accuracy, quality and final messaging.

Use case 07 of 07

Clinic Performance & Practice Growth
Grow referrals, retention and reputation with practice insights - without extra admin load.
The current reality
Building and managing a successful clinical practice requires much more than delivering high-quality patient care. Doctors need to strengthen their professional presence, build patient trust, grow referral networks, engage with their communities and manage their digital presence while ensuring their practice operates efficiently.
At the same time, clinicians need to understand where patients are coming from, what influences patient retention, how services are being utilised and where opportunities exist to improve and grow their practice.
These responsibilities require significant time, effort and expertise beyond clinical practice. Information is often spread across appointment systems, patient feedback, referral sources, online platforms and operational records, making it difficult to gain meaningful insights or take timely action while balancing busy clinical schedules.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that help doctors build, manage and grow their practice while reducing the operational effort involved in running a modern healthcare practice.
Support clinicians in strengthening their professional visibility, patient engagement, referral relationships, online presence and reputation while enabling data-driven practice growth.
Provide meaningful practice and operational insights that help improve patient acquisition, patient retention, service utilisation, communication strategies and overall practice performance.

Use case 01 of 06

Care Journey Companion
Stage-aware guidance for patients and caregivers - what next, what to prepare, when to seek help.
The current reality
Managing long-term conditions such as cancer, diabetes and maternal health can be overwhelming for patients and their caregivers. Beyond consultations, patients often struggle to understand what comes next in their care journey, how to prepare for upcoming appointments, investigations or treatments, what lifestyle changes to follow and when to seek medical attention.
Every stage of the care journey presents different information needs. Patients undergoing chemotherapy, individuals living with diabetes and expectant mothers all require timely, stage-specific guidance tailored to their condition, treatment plan and evolving healthcare needs.
Without timely, personalised and reliable guidance, patients frequently rely on fragmented information from multiple sources, leading to anxiety, confusion, poor preparedness and inconsistent adherence to care plans.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that act as an intelligent care companion, providing patients and caregivers with timely, personalised and context-aware guidance throughout their healthcare journey.
Adapt guidance based on an individual's condition, stage of care and evolving needs, helping patients understand what to expect, how to prepare, what actions are required and when to seek appropriate medical advice.
Support intuitive and accessible care journey navigation, personalised education, milestone tracking, caregiver engagement and ongoing communication, while complementing - not replacing - the relationship between patients and their healthcare providers.

Use case 02 of 06

Long-term Care Engagement
Sustained adherence over months and years - milestones, lifestyle and motivation that last.
The current reality
Successfully managing long-term conditions such as cancer, diabetes and maternal health requires patients to remain actively engaged in their care over weeks, months and, in many cases, years. Beyond taking medicines, patients need to complete investigations, attend follow-up appointments, adopt lifestyle changes, monitor their condition, recognise warning signs and actively participate in their care journey.
Maintaining long-term engagement is often challenging. Motivation may decline over time, life circumstances change, and patients can gradually lose track of investigations, treatment milestones, medications and recommended lifestyle modifications.
Family members and caregivers play an important role in supporting patients, but may not always have the information, tools or support needed to help patients stay on track. As a result, patients may gradually disengage from their care, leading to poor adherence, delayed interventions and suboptimal health outcomes.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that help patients and caregivers remain actively engaged throughout the entire care journey, supporting long-term participation rather than isolated interactions.
Encourage sustained adherence to treatment plans, investigations, lifestyle modifications, self-monitoring and other care activities by adapting to the patient's condition, stage of care, progress and individual circumstances.
Strengthen long-term connections between patients, caregivers and healthcare teams through personalised engagement, timely support and meaningful participation in the care journey.

Use case 03 of 06

Family & Caregiver Support
Tools for families who coordinate care - with consent, clarity and less stress.
The current reality
Family members and caregivers play a vital role in supporting patients with long-term conditions such as cancer, diabetes and maternal health. They often help manage appointments, medications, lifestyle modifications, home care, transportation, finances and emotional well-being, while serving as an important link between patients and healthcare providers.
Despite their central role, caregivers often have limited visibility into the patient's care journey. They may not fully understand treatment plans, upcoming milestones, warning signs, care instructions or changes in the patient's condition, making it difficult to provide timely and informed support.
Communication is frequently fragmented across patients, caregivers and healthcare providers. As care becomes more complex, particularly during prolonged treatment or recovery, the lack of coordinated information and communication can increase stress for both patients and caregivers and impact continuity of care.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that empower family members and caregivers to actively participate in the patient's healthcare journey while respecting patient consent, privacy and existing clinical workflows.
Enable caregivers to stay informed about the patient's care plan, appointments, medications, treatment milestones, care requirements and other relevant updates to provide timely and coordinated support.
Strengthen communication, care coordination and shared care planning between patients, caregivers and healthcare providers through personalised education, task management, timely alerts and other collaborative care capabilities.

Use case 04 of 06

Healthcare Navigation & Access
Clear next steps across registration, tests, billing and referrals - especially for complex journeys.
The current reality
Accessing healthcare often involves navigating multiple touchpoints across the care journey, including registration, appointments, investigations, consultations, pharmacies, billing, referrals and follow-up care. Understanding where to go, what to do next and how to complete each step of care can be confusing and overwhelming, particularly for elderly patients, individuals with chronic conditions, people from rural areas and those with limited health literacy.
For patients with long-term conditions such as cancer, diabetes and maternal health, care is often delivered across multiple departments, facilities and healthcare professionals over an extended period, making the care journey even more complex.
Patients and caregivers frequently rely on verbal instructions, paper documents and multiple sources of information to navigate the healthcare system, increasing the risk of missed steps, unnecessary delays, fragmented care experiences and poor access to services.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that help patients and caregivers navigate the healthcare system with greater confidence by providing clear, personalised and context-aware guidance throughout their care journey.
Support patients across registrations, appointments, investigations, consultations, hospital processes, referrals and follow-up care, making healthcare services easier to access, understand and navigate.
Improve the overall patient experience through multilingual communication, accessibility, document and process guidance, care coordination and proactive assistance, while reducing barriers to accessing timely healthcare services.

Use case 06 of 06

Patient Health Journey Progress
A simple longitudinal view of milestones done, pending and next - so progress is visible.
The current reality
For patients living with long-term conditions such as cancer, diabetes and maternal health, healthcare is a continuous journey involving consultations, investigations, treatments, medications, follow-up visits and preventive care over an extended period.
While every interaction generates valuable information, patients rarely have a simple, consolidated and longitudinal view of their overall health journey, making it difficult to understand how they are progressing over time.
As care spans multiple providers, facilities and digital systems, patients often struggle to understand what has been completed, which milestones have been achieved, what remains pending and what comes next. This lack of visibility can make it difficult for patients and caregivers to stay informed, prepare for upcoming stages of care and remain actively engaged throughout their healthcare journey.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that provide patients and caregivers with a clear, longitudinal and easy-to-understand view of their healthcare journey and progress over time.
Consolidate information from across the care journey to present completed milestones, ongoing treatments, investigations, preventive care, upcoming activities and future care milestones in an intuitive and meaningful manner.
Help patients visualise their progress, understand treatment pathways, track health goals and prepare for the next stage of care, empowering them to become informed, confident and active participants in managing their health.

Use case 05 of 06

Financial & Administrative Support
Insurance, schemes and paperwork made navigable - so cost does not block care.
The current reality
Patients and caregivers managing long-term conditions such as cancer, diabetes and maternal health often face significant financial and administrative challenges alongside their medical care, including navigating insurance policies, reimbursement processes, government health schemes, financial assistance programmes and hospital documentation.
Eligibility criteria, required documentation, approval processes and application timelines often vary across providers and schemes, making these processes complex, time-consuming and difficult to understand during an already stressful period.
Many patients are unaware of the financial benefits and support they may be entitled to or struggle to complete administrative processes without repeated visits, multiple interactions and extensive paperwork, leading to delays in care, higher out-of-pocket expenses and underutilisation of available support.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that simplify how patients and caregivers access financial assistance, navigate healthcare-related administrative processes and receive the support they are entitled to with greater confidence and ease.
Help patients understand available insurance benefits, reimbursement pathways, government health schemes and financial assistance programmes relevant to their individual circumstances while simplifying process navigation and decision-making.
Support eligibility guidance, document management, application assistance, status tracking, multilingual communication and personalised administrative guidance, making financial and administrative support more accessible, transparent and patient-friendly.

Healthcare Navigation & Access
Clear next steps across registration, tests, billing and referrals - especially for complex journeys.
The current reality
Accessing healthcare often involves navigating multiple touchpoints across the care journey, including registration, appointments, investigations, consultations, pharmacies, billing, referrals and follow-up care. Understanding where to go, what to do next and how to complete each step of care can be confusing and overwhelming, particularly for elderly patients, individuals with chronic conditions, people from rural areas and those with limited health literacy.
For patients with long-term conditions such as cancer, diabetes and maternal health, care is often delivered across multiple departments, facilities and healthcare professionals over an extended period, making the care journey even more complex.
Patients and caregivers frequently rely on verbal instructions, paper documents and multiple sources of information to navigate the healthcare system, increasing the risk of missed steps, unnecessary delays, fragmented care experiences and poor access to services.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that help patients and caregivers navigate the healthcare system with greater confidence by providing clear, personalised and context-aware guidance throughout their care journey.
Support patients across registrations, appointments, investigations, consultations, hospital processes, referrals and follow-up care, making healthcare services easier to access, understand and navigate.
Improve the overall patient experience through multilingual communication, accessibility, document and process guidance, care coordination and proactive assistance, while reducing barriers to accessing timely healthcare services.

Use case 03 of 06

Family & Caregiver Support
Tools for families who coordinate care - with consent, clarity and less stress.
The current reality
Family members and caregivers play a vital role in supporting patients with long-term conditions such as cancer, diabetes and maternal health. They often help manage appointments, medications, lifestyle modifications, home care, transportation, finances and emotional well-being, while serving as an important link between patients and healthcare providers.
Despite their central role, caregivers often have limited visibility into the patient's care journey. They may not fully understand treatment plans, upcoming milestones, warning signs, care instructions or changes in the patient's condition, making it difficult to provide timely and informed support.
Communication is frequently fragmented across patients, caregivers and healthcare providers. As care becomes more complex, particularly during prolonged treatment or recovery, the lack of coordinated information and communication can increase stress for both patients and caregivers and impact continuity of care.
What we're looking for
Design solutions that empower family members and caregivers to actively participate in the patient's healthcare journey while respecting patient consent, privacy and existing clinical workflows.
Enable caregivers to stay informed about the patient's care plan, appointments, medications, treatment milestones, care requirements and other relevant updates to provide timely and coordinated support.
Strengthen communication, care coordination and shared care planning between patients, caregivers and healthcare providers through personalised education, task management, timely alerts and other collaborative care capabilities.
