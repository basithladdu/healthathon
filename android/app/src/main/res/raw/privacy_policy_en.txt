# Your care information
Privacy policy · Updated 21 November 2026
Sahara — A Palliative Care Companion is provided by WEDEVIT PRIVATE LIMITED, the team behind Sahara. This policy covers the Sahara Android app, its website workspaces and the voluntary feedback and hospital-survey forms. The older browser care workspaces keep their own local records. The shared care workspace signs into the same Sahara account-backed records as Android; it does not automatically import that older browser workspace or unsigned-in phone records.
# Who is responsible for your data
WEDEVIT PRIVATE LIMITED, the team behind Sahara, decides why and how your personal data is used in Sahara. Under India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, that makes us the data fiduciary. The service providers listed below process data for us; they do not decide how it is used.
# Who can use Sahara
You must be 18 or over to create a Sahara account. If the patient is a child, a parent or lawful guardian must set up and manage their records and give any consent for them.
# What we use and why
The app keeps the names, care conversations, reviewed notes, confirmations, prescribed-medicine details, visits, tasks, lab results, conversation requests, symptom notes, check-ins and journal entries you enter. We use them to show your care records, organise daily care and, when you sign in, share records with people you invite.
Without signing in, these records stay in the app’s private storage on your phone. If you sign in, names, published care notes, confirmations, named tasks and daily entries you add in that account are also stored with Supabase for syncing and sharing. Conversation drafts and their original words or reviewed sections are uploaded when you explicitly save or send them to the shared clinician workspace. An unsent phone draft is not automatically uploaded. Existing unsigned-in records are not automatically imported into the account.
Supabase Auth processes your account email and password for sign-in. The app stores an encrypted sign-in token, not your password. Our service providers also receive network information, such as your IP address, when your phone connects to them. Their service logs can include your IP address, an approximate city or country inferred from it, and request diagnostics used to keep the service working and secure. This is not GPS tracking. If you email support, we use your email and message to handle your request.
# Personal symptom notes
A symptom note can contain the symptom, a number from zero to ten or an unanswered rating, observation date and time, reporting period, who answered, author name, pain location or triggers, reported change and optional words. Comparisons use earlier entries for the same person, symptom and reporting context. They do not diagnose a condition, calculate a clinical risk score or decide treatment. This personal diary is not a validated clinical questionnaire.
In Android, these notes use the existing care-record store. Unsigned-in notes stay on the phone; notes saved in a signed-in account also go to Supabase and follow that person’s existing sharing permissions. The older browser diary remains in its separate browser workspace. Sharing an export sends the selected words to the app and recipient you choose.
# Microphone and recordings
Microphone permission is requested when you choose to record. Ask everyone taking part for permission first. You can stop at any time; recording also stops when the app leaves the foreground. Standard recordings remain on your phone and are not transcribed automatically. Goals-of-care recordings can be sent to an online AI speech service, but only after consent; see “Goals-of-care recordings and AI drafts” below. Saving or sending a text draft to the shared clinician workspace uploads that text to your account; it does not upload its recording.
Optional turn-by-turn dictation uses Android’s on-device recognizer on supported phones. You choose the speaker and language and check the words; speaker identity is not detected or verified. There is no cloud speech fallback in this on-device dictation flow. Recognised words remain in the draft until you explicitly save or send them to the shared workspace or publish a reviewed care-note version. Read aloud uses an installed offline voice and does not translate the text.
From Android version 0.6.0, you can also choose to transcribe a saved, consented recording with Qwen3-ASR running locally through sherpa-onnx. This requires a separate approximately 987 MB language-pack download, a 64-bit process and sufficient free memory. Download requests go to Hugging Face and its content-delivery hosts, which receive network information such as your IP address and the model file requested. Downloading the model does not send your recording or care text to them. After the download, transcription runs on the phone without a cloud fallback.
The original recording, recognised words, segment offsets and reviewed corrections stay with that recording in private phone storage. Check the words against the audio before using them in a care note; the model can miss mixed-language words, names or numbers and does not identify speakers. Removing the recording through Sahara also removes its saved local transcript. If you choose to save recognised words in a signed-in care record or shared draft, that text follows the account sharing rules above; the original audio is not uploaded with it.
The fictional doctor preview keeps its sample records, edited drafts, preview recordings and app feedback on this phone, separately from Sahara accounts. Changing or deleting a Sahara account does not clear this preview. Only the newest preview recording is shown; older recordings are not listed, and preview recordings cannot currently be deleted individually in the app. Clear Sahara app data in Android settings or uninstall the app to remove the local preview data. This also clears other Sahara information stored on that phone. Preview PDFs are created in the app cache, which Android may clear. Copies you export or share remain with their recipients until they remove them.
# Goals-of-care recordings and AI drafts
A doctor can record a goals-of-care conversation in Sahara. Before recording, the doctor ticks that everyone present agrees to the conversation being recorded and written down. The screen says that the audio is sent to an AI service to transcribe it. Without that tick, nothing is recorded or sent.
The phone then uploads the audio in parts to an online AI speech service. Today this is Google Gemini. The audio goes straight from the phone to Google; the request passes through Sahara’s website server on Vercel. We ask Google to delete each uploaded part once it is transcribed, and Google removes uploaded files after 48 hours in any case. Google may process this data outside India. When we switch it on, Sahara can also send the audio to Sarvam AI, an Indian company, for transcription. Google Gemini stays the fallback, and a short text-only Gemini step labels who spoke (doctor or patient and family).
The AI then reads the transcript and drafts a goals-of-care document, with a word-for-word quote for each item. The draft is a suggestion. The doctor reviews it, edits it and signs it before it is used. The transcript and the AI’s reading are stored in Supabase under the signed-in account, and only that account can read them. The original recording stays on the phone.
You do not have to use the online service. Transcription on the phone and typing the conversation remain available, and Sahara falls back to them if the online service cannot finish.
# Translation
Android offers optional Hindi–English machine translation using Google ML Kit on-device models. An initial language-pack download needs a connection to Google; translation of care text then runs on the phone. ML Kit does not send the input care text or translated output to Google for translation. Technical SDK metrics and configured language settings are handled as described below. The original remains visible beside the translation. Check the result with a bilingual person before relying on it; machine translation is not a clinical interpretation.
You can also ask for a care note to be translated online, including into other Indian languages. This sends the note’s text, after you agree, to Sarvam AI when it is switched on, or otherwise to the AI service named on the screen (currently Google Gemini). The translation is marked for review, and the original stays beside it.
# Shared drafts, clinician review and care-team tasks
In the shared workspace, the record owner chooses family or clinician access, the permitted record categories, whether changes are allowed and when membership expires. Invitations are private, single-use and expire after three days; the owner can cancel an unused invitation or revoke a member. Expired or revoked permission blocks future service requests but cannot recall previously saved or exported copies. An exact-version link or QR code identifies a note; it does not bypass sign-in or grant public access.
A clinician-verification request stores the applicant’s registered name, medical council, registration number, request status, revision and review timestamps. A separately authorised verifier records the checked source, decision and reason. Submitting a request or selecting a doctor view does not grant approval or clinical access. The applicant can read their own request; authorised verification staff can review it. These details are not a public clinician directory. A patient’s separate invitation and current permissions are also required for a verified clinician to review a shared conversation.
Shared drafts keep the original source, sections, author account, revision and submission state. A clinician review saves a distinct note version with the reviewing account, checked draft revision and source hash. Patient and family confirmations are separate, account-attributed events tied to the exact note version; family confirmation follows the patient record owner’s confirmation. These events record a discussion and acknowledgment, not verified understanding, legal consent or a treatment order.
Named tasks store the author and recipient accounts, title, details, chosen due time, acceptance or completion state and timestamps. Only the assigned account can accept or complete the task. Workflow counts reflect these saved events; they do not measure patient outcomes or clinical effectiveness. Shared changes do not themselves dispatch a call, email or emergency alert.
# Feedback and hospital surveys
You can share the app feedback form without signing in. If you choose to send it, Sahara stores your rating, self-described role, topic, optional comments, consent and submission receipt in its Supabase database to improve the app. Please do not include patient names, phone numbers, ABHA numbers or medical details.
The voluntary hospital survey requires only a hospital name and your consent. You may optionally share your professional name, role, registering council, registration number and state, plus a professional email. Incomplete or imperfectly formatted details are retained for review, rather than treated as verified. The current form does not request a mobile number. These professional and contact details are stored separately from hospital-service information and are not for public display. Optional contacts are used to follow up about your submission, not for marketing. We do not verify your identity, registration, contact ownership or hospital affiliation through this form, and submitting does not send an OTP, email or SMS.
Hospital-service information includes the hospital name and address, institution type, your connection to it, state, city, an optional confirmed hospital map pin, names and departments of palliative-care physicians, palliative-care services, morphine availability and any note for families. All details beyond the hospital name are optional. An unchecked map pin is not saved. The pin is a place you select, not your current GPS location. Submissions remain pending review, including any named doctors; they do not automatically create a public listing or a verified professional profile. Reviewed hospital-service information may be used in the care directory. Do not enter patient or family contact details.
On the website, place search sends your query and IP address to Photon. Opening the hospital map sends the viewed area and IP address to OpenStreetMap. The website uses a strictly necessary, HttpOnly feedback cookie lasting 24 hours to limit repeated submissions. Only a keyed hash of that random token is stored with the receipt, not the raw token or IP address. Hosting and Supabase service logs may contain network diagnostics.
Restricted service administration and authorised directory reviewers can read hospital submissions and contributor details; app-feedback records are limited to service administration. There is no public submission list. An approval publishes a separate edited hospital listing with a dated source check and recheck date, without contributor contacts or registration identifiers. Withdrawal or expiry removes that listing from the reviewed directory. Internal review decisions, evidence references and revision history remain private. We retain these records for app improvement and hospital-directory review. There is currently no automatic expiry or self-service deletion for these voluntary submissions. For a correction or deletion, email workwithdevit@gmail.com with your receipt if available, or enough details to locate the submission. We verify the request before changing or deleting it. Closing a Sahara account does not automatically remove a separate, unsigned feedback or survey submission. Separate on-phone feedback is not sent by these website forms.
# Prescription and medicine photo organiser
Cloud photo scanning remains off. The Read prescription flow reads printed English/Latin and Hindi/Devanagari text on your device: Android uses bundled ML Kit text models. The website runs Tesseract in a browser worker using engine and language files served by Sahara for English text. The selected photos and their recognised words are not sent to an AI service, Supabase or another OCR service. Handwriting, small print and other scripts may need manual entry. You can always add medicines by hand.
Google’s Android ML Kit SDK sends technical diagnostics and usage metrics to Google over HTTPS, including app and device information, a per-installation identifier, performance, input/output sizes, API settings and feature events or errors. Translation also reports configured source and destination languages and uses Firebase remote configuration and installation services for configuration diagnostics. ML Kit initialises with the Android app; Sahara does not offer a setting to turn off these technical metrics. They may be sent when Sahara starts, even if you do not use OCR or translation. These metrics do not include the selected image, recognised words or care text. See Google’s ML Kit data disclosure and ML Kit’s privacy terms. Website OCR uses Sahara-hosted engine files; ordinary hosting logs can record the request for those files and your IP address.
The reader supplies possible prescription lines for review. It does not identify medicines from packaging, verify a brand match, calculate doses, choose treatment or infer a clock time from a frequency such as twice daily. Compare the words, name, strength, dose, food instruction, course dates and any reminder times against the original before saving. Corrections are separate from the original reading. Only records you explicitly confirm are added to the selected person’s care list.
Website prescription and packaging images you save with the reviewed list stay in this browser’s IndexedDB so you can view the original again; clearing the site data removes them. Android keeps saved source copies within the person’s private report files. Shared prescription originals remain while medicines can refer to them; removing the person’s records, clearing app data or uninstalling removes those copies. If you are signed in, copied medicine text or calendar entries you confirm may sync with your shared care records, while the original files remain local. Cloud scanning will not be enabled without suitable provider terms, an updated explanation and consent before an upload.
# Reports you add
From version 0.4.0, you can choose a PDF or photo using Android’s file picker. Sahara saves a private copy for the selected person and account. It does not read other files or upload report files to Supabase or an AI service. Only text you choose to send with a question leaves the phone (see below). Added reports stay on that phone until you remove them, remove the person’s records or uninstall the app. Keep your original files. Removing a report in Sahara does not delete the original.
If you choose Read and copy facts, Android can render up to the first twelve PDF pages or read an image with on-device OCR. The website reads PDF text directly and uses local browser OCR for scanned pages and photos. The original remains available beside the text. Correct the reading and confirm it against the original before it becomes searchable. Android keeps the original file hash, page, line and checked excerpt with a copied fact; website passages cite the selected report, page when available and text line. Search returns matching source passages, not medical advice or an inferred diagnosis. No supported passage means no answer. Unreadable or unprocessed pages are identified; the reader does not claim to have checked the full file.
Reviewed text and corrections are stored with that account’s private report review on Android and with the website’s separate local document workspace. Removing a report or person’s private records removes its Android review text. If you explicitly copy a fact into a signed-in care record, that copy follows the care-record sharing and deletion rules above; removing its source report does not silently remove previously confirmed care entries.
You can ask a question about your own reports. Sahara first looks for matching lines on the phone. If you also want an online answer, you tick a box each time to agree, and the app sends your question and the text read from the chosen reports (never the photos or files) to the AI service named on the screen (currently Google Gemini). The answer must point to lines in your reports, and you check it against them. It does not diagnose or advise on medicines.
# Medicine and follow-up reminders
Android reminders are optional. Sahara asks for notification permission when you enable them and keeps your selected times, course boundaries and next scheduled alerts in private storage on the phone. Only the active account sends alerts. Saved edits, completed or removed activities, account changes, phone restarts and clock or time-zone changes reconcile the next alerts. Medicine wall-clock times follow the phone’s current time zone; dated visits retain their saved instant. No reminder information is sent to an advertising or notification provider.
Notifications use a general Sahara reminder message without medicine names, doses or patient names on the lock screen. Opening one returns to Sahara; it does not mark a medicine as taken. Permission denial leaves the care record intact, and you can return to notification settings. Android may delay or block alerts because of battery restrictions, a disabled channel, force-stop or a switched-off phone. These are inexact reminders, not an urgent-care or guaranteed medication-administration service. The website’s calendar alone cannot send an alert after its page is closed.
If a medicine has not been marked taken after several reminders over at least a day, the phone may show one gentle “missed medicine” notification (at most once in three days for each medicine). This is worked out on the phone and is not sent to us or to anyone else.
# Who can see your records
Signed-in record owners can create invitations with read or edit permission. Each invitation can be used once and expires after three days. The owner can see an invited member’s email and remove their permission. Removing permission stops future online requests; it cannot recall copies already saved.
When you share reports or export care notes, Android lets you choose another app and recipient. Those copies leave Sahara and follow that service’s policies. Check the contents and recipient before sharing. Names and roles entered in the legacy walkthrough are self-entered. In the shared workspace, a clinician request remains pending until an authorised independent verifier checks the professional register and the applicant’s identity/control. That review does not certify treatment decisions, create a legal signature or replace the patient’s separate invitation.
# Emergency QR card and web link
The printed emergency card is plain text; anyone who scans it can read it. If the record owner also turns on the emergency web link, a copy of the card is kept on our server: the person’s name, their latest confirmed goals of care, emergency contacts and medicines. Anyone who has the link or QR code can open that copy without signing in, read-only. It stops working when the owner turns it off, makes a new link, or after 365 days. We count how many times it is opened and when it was last opened. Only the person who added the profile can manage the link.
# Community
Signed-in members can join groups, write posts and replies, react, save posts and report posts or replies. Other members see the community name you choose, never your email or phone number. Posts cannot contain links, email addresses or phone numbers. Please do not share patient details. The Sahara team can read reports and hide or restore posts and replies. A post reported by three different members is hidden until the team looks at it. You can remove your own posts and replies.
# Centre suggestions and morphine reports
You can suggest a palliative care centre that is missing from the directory. After a dose of oral morphine is marked taken, the app may also ask (at most once a month for each medicine) where the morphine came from: a centre from the directory, or a pharmacy or hospital you type. Only the place is stored. A morphine report is not linked to your account: the server keeps only a one-way code of it to limit reports per day, which cannot be turned back into your account. Centre suggestions are linked to your account. Both are private to the Sahara team. Nothing is public. Only centres the team has checked are recognised institutions may be shown to other families, in a later directory release; places you type are never published as morphine sources.
# Living will, care plan and wellbeing check
The living will (advance medical directive) and care plan you prepare in Sahara are kept in private storage on the phone. They are not sent to our server. If an emergency card is issued, it carries only the status, date, version and attesting officer of the advance care plan. The app prepares the paper; it becomes valid only when signed and attested as the law requires. The caregiver wellbeing check and its private journal also stay on the phone.
# Maps and links
Android version 0.6.0 includes 1,631 published directory listings across 36 Indian states and union territories. This directory snapshot was updated on 20 October 2026; individual listings show their source-check dates. These public listings do not confirm current services, opening hours or medicine stock. Call the centre before travelling. Searching this bundled list does not use your GPS location or upload your care records.
OpenFreeMap supplies map tiles through MapLibre and receives your IP address and the map area requested. Sahara does not request your precise GPS location. Calls, directions and source links open your dialler, maps or browser when you select them. Those services handle information under their own policies. The website’s MapLibre maps use OpenStreetMap tiles; that provider receives your IP address and the map areas requested.
# How information is protected
Sahara has no advertising SDK. We do not sell care information or use it for advertising. ML Kit’s technical usage and diagnostic metrics are described above. Connections to Sahara’s service, Supabase, Google model and SDK services, Hugging Face model hosts and OpenFreeMap use HTTPS. Sign-in tokens are encrypted using Android Keystore. Account caches are separated from each other and from unsigned-in records, and app records are excluded from Android backup and transfer.
Protect your phone with a screen lock. The app’s records rely on Android’s storage protections; its database does not have separate app-level encryption. Supabase processes account and shared care information to provide the service.
If a data breach affects your personal data, we will tell the people affected and the Data Protection Board of India, as the law requires.

# Keeping and deleting information
Unsigned-in records remain until you remove them, clear app data or uninstall. Signed-in records remain online after uninstalling. Record owners can choose More → Remove this person’s records to delete those records online and clear the corresponding app cache. You can also delete individual recordings.
To delete your Sahara account and associated information, use Your account → Delete account in the app. This deletes it straight away. If the app cannot finish, request deletion here. For an email request, we verify control of the registered email before processing, explain the expected completion date and confirm by email when complete. Opening the page or an email draft does not delete anything.
Deleting in the app removes your unfinished shared drafts, your clinician-verification request, the care records you own and your memberships, and then removes your sign-in. Removing the sign-in also deletes what is linked to it: goals-of-care transcripts and AI readings, community profile, posts, replies, reactions, saves and reports, centre suggestions, morphine reports, emergency links and app feedback.
Withdrawing consent: you can untick or decline any online AI step at any time, and Sahara keeps working on the phone. To stop sharing, remove a family member or doctor in Your account, or turn off the emergency link. Signing out stops new data being sent. Withdrawing consent does not undo processing that already happened, but you can delete the data as described here.
Deletion removes your Sahara account details, care records you own, invitations for those records and your membership in other people’s care records. Records owned by someone else are not removed by closing your account. Exported files and copies saved by recipients must be removed separately. If the same sign-in is used for another service, we handle Sahara data separately and explain any shared account details before completing the request.
If another service still needs your sign-in, we keep only its account ID and the Sahara closure date to keep Sahara access closed. This marker is removed when that shared sign-in is deleted. In that case, the items linked only to the sign-in (listed above) also stay until it is deleted; email us to remove them sooner.
Closure removes active staff permissions, your clinician-verification request, assistance-quota identifiers and unfinished drafts you authored. Open tasks assigned to the closed account are cancelled, not marked completed. Reviewed source conversations, care notes, confirmations and task history that belong to another patient remain part of that patient’s record. Decision audit evidence supporting those recorded reviews or professional approvals can also remain; closing an account does not rewrite another patient’s history.
We keep information while it is needed to provide your account and care records. Online records are kept while your account is open and deleted when the account is deleted; there is no automatic expiry today. We keep support correspondence while handling your request. If any information must be retained for a legal or security reason, we will tell you what is kept, why and for how long when handling your deletion request.
# About care decisions
Sahara supports care and record-keeping. It does not diagnose, prescribe or decide treatment, and it does not replace advice from your care team. A doctor reviews every AI draft before it is used. Saved goals-of-care notes record a conversation; they are not treatment orders or legal directives.
