Built inside Indian law

Legal review

A legal reviewer checked our goals-of-care form, and the app follows India's data and end-of-life law.

1The lawyer's review of the goals-of-care form 31 Oct 2026

  • Ask both sex and gender in the patient details.
  • Family includes live-in and queer relationships.
  • Record who was tried but could not be reached.
  • Primary and secondary decision makers, each reachable 24 hours.
  • Capacity: add guidance on how, and when, to assess it.
  • Withdrawal and withholding follows Common Cause v Union of India (2018, 2023): board dates, members, decisions.
  • Witnesses for a thumb impression or limited literacy, at home and in hospital.
  • Result: the app's note prints this form word for word on Android and web, and a test checks every line.

2Advance directive law

  • The note records a conversation. It is not an advance directive or a DNAR order.
  • The living-will section helps people prepare one: under the 2023 simplification it needs two witnesses and a notary or gazetted officer.
  • Only adults (18+) can make an advance directive.

3DPDP Act 2023: consent and data

  • Two consent ticks before recording: the patient (or legal representative) and the family in the room.
  • 18+ to create an account; for a child patient, a parent or lawful guardian consents.
  • Data minimisation: report files and photos stay on the phone; recordings leave only with consent.
  • Morphine reports are not linked to anyone's account.
  • Withdraw consent, delete a person's records or the account.

For the team to confirm

  • Reviewer's name and title for print (the form review is credited to Rituparna P).
  • Whether the lawyer also advised on the DPDP points (the saved review covers the form only).
  • Server region (records say Mumbai), grievance contact and how long data is kept.
  • Nominee under DPDP s.14: not built yet.
