# Sahara journey film: script v3 (draft for the team to check)

The narrator is the whole team ("we" means all five of us). This is not the engineers talking about the doctors.

## 1. The problem (emergency room, night)
- Three in the morning. An emergency room.
- A woman with advanced cancer comes in. The doctor has never met her.
- CPR? A breathing tube? Nobody knows what she wanted. So her family has to have the hardest conversation of their lives, all over again.
- In India, most people with advanced cancer never get palliative care, and what they want at the end is almost never written down.

## 2. Who we are
- That's what Sahara is for. Five of us found each other at Health-a-thon.
- Basith and Awaiz run WeDevIT, a software company.
- Dr Sujay is from Tata IISc Medical School in Bengaluru, Dr Sharada is from AIIMS Mangalagiri, and Shirin is a final-year medical student.
- Back in August, Dr Sujay told the team: patients do say what they want. It just never gets written down, or found when it matters.

## 3. How it changed
- At first, we built it for doctors.
- Then Shirin asked one question: why would a family install this?
- She was right. So we changed it, one switch at a time.
- Doctor screens became an app for patients and families.
- When we got shortlisted, we rebuilt it as a real Android app.
- And everything grew around one thing: the goals of care talk.

## 4. The goals of care talk
- With everyone's consent, the doctor records the talk. AI writes out who said what, even in Indian languages, and fills in a clinical document, ticking off thirteen topics from clinical guidelines.
- The doctor checks it, fixes it, and signs. Then the family signs, and it locks.
- We even got legal advice on the document's wording and format.

## 5. At home, every day
- At home, between visits, Sahara helps the family every day.
- They log symptoms on validated scales, use a distress thermometer, and mark pain on a body map. The doctor sees the trends.
- They snap a prescription or a medicine strip, AI sorts the medicines, and every dose gets a reminder.
- They ask their reports a question, and AI shows the exact line that answers it.
- We scraped over sixteen hundred palliative care centres, in every state, onto one map, including where to find oral morphine.
- And there are community groups, so no family goes through this alone.

## 6. Languages
- And families don't talk in one language. So it listens in English, Hindi, Telugu, Urdu and Kannada, even mixed in one sentence.

## 7. Back to the emergency room
- Back to that emergency room. One scan of her card, and the doctor knows what she wanted.

## 8. The caregiver (short)
- Oh, wait. Late one night, it hit us.
- We kept asking how the patient feels. Nobody was asking the daughter.
- So caregivers get their own check too, and someone to call.

## 9. The work, and the close
- Three months. We had a lot of meetings. We ran a survey of doctors and hospitals.
- We pushed almost six hundred commits to GitHub. A commit is one saved, tested change to the code.
- It's all built on free and open-source tools, on a secure server.
- And it's live on Google Play today. *(The Google Play badge and the real listing are on screen.)*
- Sahara. Care that follows what matters to the patient.
