Then we asked ourselves one question. Why would a family install all this?

So we pivoted, towards the patient and the family. And we kept one thing at the centre: the goals of care talk. That's when the doctor, the patient and the family sit down together, and decide what kind of care she wants.

With everyone's consent, the doctor records that talk. AI writes out who said what. For Indian languages, we use Sarvam AI. Then the AI checks that nothing important was missed. It goes through thirteen topics, taken from India's end-of-life care guidelines, written by the national critical care and palliative care societies.

Then the AI turns the conversation into a goals of care document. That's a clinical record of her wishes. Does she want CPR? A ventilator? Intensive care? And where does she want to be cared for?

Nothing is final until the doctor reads it, corrects it, and signs it. Then the family signs too, and it's locked.

We also took legal advice on the wording and the format of that document.

But goals of care is only the start. Sahara stays with the family, every single day.

Patients track their symptoms. They rate how they're feeling on a distress thermometer, and they mark where it hurts on a body map. Their doctor can see how it's changing over time.

There's a mood journal, for the days that are hard to put into words.

They take a photo of a prescription, or a medicine strip. AI works out the medicines, and reminds them about every dose.

They upload their reports, and ask a question in plain words. AI answers, and shows the exact line in the report it came from.

Their next hospital visit is scheduled right in the app.

Family members get their own login, so everyone stays in the loop.

There's an emergency SOS button. And a QR card that carries the patient's wishes, contacts and medicines.
