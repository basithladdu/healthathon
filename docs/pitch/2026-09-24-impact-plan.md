# Proposed 60–90 day impact plan for Saathi

This is a measurement proposal, not a pilot commitment or a result. No clinic, implementation partner, participants, baseline, or protocol has been confirmed. The team must agree eligibility, consent, recordkeeping, and review with a clinical site before collecting data.

## Count the pathway without mixing stages

Use the same clinician-approved eligibility definition before and after deployment. Report counts and denominators at every step:

1. **Eligible:** patients who meet the agreed clinical criteria during the period.
2. **Informed:** eligible patients offered clear information about the option to have a goals-of-care discussion (GOCD). Record the number offered and whether they accept or decline where the site approves this tally.
3. **Consented:** patients who choose to take part in the agreed workflow/evaluation. Consent to a discussion, to audio recording, and to use of evaluation data are separate choices; do not treat one as another.
4. **Patient-initiated:** requests made by patients (and, if the protocol includes it, family members). Count requests separately from discussions that actually happen.
5. **Doctor-initiated:** discussions a clinician starts. Count a clinician's offer separately from a discussion the patient chooses to have.
6. **Held, documented, signed:** count completed discussions, discussions with a Care Note saved, and Care Notes reviewed and signed by the doctor as distinct outcomes. A request is not a completed discussion; a completed discussion is not automatically documented or signed.

For each period, publish the funnel as `count / denominator`, including the denominator name. Report patient-initiated requests / informed eligible patients, and doctor-initiated discussions / informed eligible patients, as separate counts and rates; also show both over consented participants if that denominator is approved and consistently available. A primary service-level comparison can be **patients with a GOCD documented in the clinical record / all eligible patients**, before versus after deployment, if the site can lawfully and consistently count both periods. Also show `documented / consented participants` to describe use among participants and `doctor-signed Care Notes / documented GOCDs` to distinguish signing from documentation. Keep raw counts beside each rate so consent and reach are visible. Never combine initiation sources or stages as “AI-generated conversations.”

## Practical collection plan

- **Before deployment:** use a 2–3 week baseline with the usual clinic process, after the site approves definitions. A clinician or designated coordinator records eligible and informed counts, patient requests, clinician-initiated offers/discussions, held conversations, and documentation/signing in the usual record. If a reliable comparable baseline cannot be collected, state that clearly rather than reconstructing it from incomplete records.
- **During deployment:** for the remaining pilot period, the clinician records whether a discussion was patient- or doctor-initiated and whether it was held. The coordinator tallies the same stages weekly, checks that a saved Care Note is actually doctor-signed, and records withdrawals, missing data, and workflow failures. The app remains an aid; the clinician owns the clinical record and decisions.
- **At review:** compare the same eligibility criteria, definitions, and clinic workflow before and after deployment. Show `n/N`, dates, missing counts, and the number who declined or withdrew when approved for reporting. Do not set a success percentage in advance or infer clinical benefit from more requests or signatures alone.

Offering information about goals-of-care discussions to **all clinic patients** is only a possible opt-in communication approach to discuss with a future site. No partner is confirmed. It does not mean every patient is eligible, should have a discussion, or has consented.

No measured results, savings, clinical outcomes, adoption, or accuracy claims are available. A signed Care Note is a workflow output, not proof that preferences were understood or that care changed.
