3. Inside the guide (steps 2–7, short)

Checked against Indian law and official sources; rules vary a little by state.

Call their doctor: they confirm the death and write the cause-of-death certificate (Form 4A at home; Form 4 in hospital). A death from a known illness does not need the police.

The first day: tell close family; rent a freezer box if needed; call the cremation or burial ground and keep the receipt. Eye donation within about 6 hours; body donation, call the medical college straight away.

Leftover medicines: return morphine to the palliative team or pharmacy; do not keep, share or throw it away.

Registering the death: within 21 days, free, at the municipal office, gram panchayat or online. Ask for 5 to 10 copies.

In the weeks after: bank, insurance, family pension, legal heir certificate, Aadhaar on myAadhaar.

Looking after yourself: grief comes in waves; your palliative team can stay in touch; Tele-MANAS 14416, free, any time.

4. After a morphine dose is marked taken

Asked once, at most every 30 days per medicine. "Not now" closes it.

Could you help another family?

Oral morphine can be hard to find. If you share where you got yours, we can help families near you find it too. Your answer stays private with the Sahara team; other families only ever see the place, after we check it. This is completely up to you.
