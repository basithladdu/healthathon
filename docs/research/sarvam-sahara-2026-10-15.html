The recommendation

Make the care conversation useful after the appointment.

Use Sarvam to capture Indian-language conversations, draft a reviewable goals-of-care record and read the approved plan aloud.

Keep Sahara free for families. A hospital, hospice, NGO programme or public-health buyer can fund the staff coordination workflow. That is a buyer hypothesis to test, rather than a reason to put essential support behind a patient subscription.

Six opportunities worth prioritising

1

A reviewed goals-of-care record from a conversation

This is Sahara's distinctive core and reduces repeated explanations across visits.
For
Patient, family and palliative clinician
Flow
Consent to record → capture the conversation → edit transcript and confirm speakers → draft supported goals, understanding, preferences, decisions and open questions → clinician approves → patient sees the same dated version.
What the model does
Source-constrained structuring, with each material statement linked to its transcript excerpt and speaker.
Review and boundary
Do not infer treatment limits, capacity, prognosis, DNR status or legal directives. Unknown and disputed statements stay visible. Approval is versioned; a later change needs a new review.
Proposed phase
Core clinical pilot after data agreement and clinician-led evaluation

2

Listen to the approved plan

Useful when reading is tiring or the caregiver is more comfortable in another language.
For
Patient and caregiver
Flow
Open the clinician-approved plan → choose a tested language → listen, pause or replay a short section; keep the original text available.
What the model does
Reviewed translation followed by speech synthesis; cache audio against the approved plan version.
Review and boundary
A bilingual clinical reviewer checks important numbers, medicine names and negation before patient-facing use. No invented expansion of instructions.
Proposed phase
Start with approved general education; then personal plans after validation

3

A voice diary that prepares the next visit

Replaces a long form and helps families remember what changed.
For
Patient or caregiver
Flow
Speak a short update → confirm what was heard → save the dated note → create a source-linked visit brief and questions for the care team.
What the model does
Transcription, confirmed fact extraction and a chronological summary of the person's own reports.
Review and boundary
Label these entries patient-reported, separately from clinician-approved records. Do not turn self-reports into a diagnosis, predicted risk or a promise that a clinician is monitoring them. Display the agreed human contact route separately.
Proposed phase
Early assisted-record pilot; Pood is only a prototype precedent

4

A practical caregiver handover

Reduces repeated calls when a different family member takes over.
For
Family caregivers and home-care nurses
Flow
Choose an approved care-plan version → dictate completed practical tasks and open questions → confirm owners → share a concise handover with permission.
What the model does
Turns supported statements into a short, attributed checklist; read aloud if needed.
Review and boundary
Never silently assign medicines, alter administration instructions or identify the decision maker from a voice label. Real sharing requires authenticated roles and permission checks.

After authenticated sharing is verified

Ask for care without completing another long form

A clear support task that complements Sahara's MapLibre centres map.
For
Family seeking a palliative service
Flow
Speak the location, language and access needs → confirm minimal details → search a maintained centre directory → show actual contact options and a draft enquiry.
What the model does
Intent and location extraction, plain-language explanations and optionally spoken navigation.

6

Teach-back support for the nurse

Checks whether communication worked while preserving the human conversation.
For
Nurse with patient or caregiver
Flow
Use an approved explanation → ask the person to describe it in their words → show a transcript beside the plan → nurse clarifies misunderstandings.
What the model does
Transcribe the response beside the approved explanation so the nurse can compare them. Automated highlighting of possible gaps is a later research option, requiring high-consequence omission and false-alarm evaluation.
Review and boundary
No pass/fail score for competence, consent or adherence; only a clinician can judge understanding and next steps.
Proposed phase
Later supervised usability study

How each Sarvam piece fits

Speech recognition · Saaras

Converts audio into editable text; batch diarisation separates speaker labels.

It does not know who a speaker is, validate a clinical statement or make a diagnosis.

Saaras speech-recognition model ↗

Generative drafting · Sarvam-105B

Organises supplied text into a draft, extracts supported fields and rewrites approved content.

It does not supply a clinical evidence base, reliable citations or approved treatment by itself. Sahara must supply retrieval, validation and review.

Translation · Sarvam Translate / Mayura

Converts approved text between the selected supported languages.

It does not guarantee preservation of clinical negation, numbers or medicine names. Check the exact language/model pair.

Speech synthesis · Bulbul

Reads an approved text aloud using a supported preset voice.

Natural speech is not proof of a correct care plan. Use the approved version and make generated narration clear.

Before a supervised clinical pilot

Synthetic language benchmark

Use fictional consultations with code-switching, weak audio, overlapping speech, negation, names, numbers and corrections. Get bilingual clinical annotations; record critical errors, not just average word error.

Clinical draft review

Count unsupported statements, omitted preferences, wrong speaker attribution and incorrect certainty. A material uncorrected clinical error blocks that output from sharing.

Small supervised partner pilot

Agree patient consent, participant roles, storage, retention and an escalation contact with a palliative partner. Compare current workflow with reviewed drafts; do not launch unattended medical advice.

Useful operational measures

Measure staff review minutes, correction burden, family plan retrieval, comprehension interviews, source traceability and completion of staff-assigned follow-up. Clinical benefit is not established by a faster transcript.

Cost and continuity

Measure actual minutes, characters, tokens and storage per active family. Cap retries, cache approved audio, retain manual entry and recover work when credits expire or the network fails.

Data agreement and implementation boundaries
Patient data
Sarvam's current terms apply a DPA for customer processing. They require professional involvement/review for tailored medical advice. The public privacy policy excludes enterprise content governed by customer agreements; it is not evidence of the exact retention, training or hosting terms for this account. Confirm those terms and healthcare scope before identifiable patient input.
Backend
API credentials stay server-side. Build real authentication, file permissions, job state, cancellation, retry/deduplication, deletion and audit history. Room persistence alone does not establish care-team sync.
Language coverage
Use the clinic's actual languages and test model/voice pairs. Do not advertise one uniform 22-language clinical voice experience; API coverage differs. Current docs recommend Saaras v4 and Bulbul v4-flash for new integrations, with v3 still relevant to some TTS languages.
Current scanner
Existing public photo scanning remains off. This research does not activate Sarvam, replace the scanner provider or change current release declarations.

Financial sustainability

The family uses it. The care organisation funds it.

Sell documentation and coordination to the organisation already responsible for care. Keep the family's core app free.

These are proposed buyer models. No willingness to pay, contract, grant, tender or sponsor commitment has been established.

Best first commercial hypothesis

Cancer hospitals and palliative-care services

What they fund
Staff documentation, reviewed care-plan sharing, handovers, role permissions, audit history and integration/support.
Commercial structure to test
Annual or monthly institution licence with a defined active-family/usage allowance; setup and integration scoped separately.
Why this could fit
Less duplicate documentation and fewer communication gaps. Validate actual saved staff time and budget with buyers before setting a price.
Strong mission-aligned pilot hypothesis

Hospices, charitable hospitals and palliative NGOs

What they fund
Coordination for home-care teams and multilingual family communication, funded from an agreed project budget.
Commercial structure to test
A partner or donor underwrites a capped cohort; the family does not buy a subscription.
Why this could fit
Supports existing free services without adding a new patient fee.
Partner-led funding hypothesis; eligibility and contract need checking

Corporate CSR or philanthropic sponsors through an eligible care partner

What they fund
A defined deployment, training, support and an aggregate activity report with agreed privacy controls.
Commercial structure to test
Time-limited project funding with a continuation plan. Do not assume a for-profit company can receive CSR grants as an eligible implementing agency.
Why this could fit
Makes a specific free-care programme more accessible; Karunashraya publicly offers both free care and CSR participation.

Protect the model from bad incentives

  • Avoid charging families to read their own approved care record or access essential support.
  • Avoid referral commissions that bias which care service a family sees.
  • Avoid advertising against sensitive conversations or selling patient data.
  • Avoid an unlimited voice-call promise priced only from expiring sponsor credits.

First buyer conversations

Start with the assigned clinician/mentor and a palliative service lead. Ask who owns documentation, who reviews drafts, who has a software budget, which work takes time today, which languages matter and what a funded pilot must prove. No outreach was sent for this research.
