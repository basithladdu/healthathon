# people: patient Joseph (74); wife Mary and son Kevin (family); doctor

D: Joseph, Mary, Kevin, thanks for coming in. Your breathing has been harder these last months.
P: It has. I can't climb the stairs at all now.
D: Tell me, what is the thing you care about most right now?
P: Getting to church on Sundays. And sitting with Mary in the evenings without gasping. <<Q1>>
D: When you think about the treatment we give you, what would you like it to do for you?
P: Just keep me comfortable. I'm not looking for miracles. <<Q2>>
F: Dad, the new inhaler could still help.
P: Maybe, but comfort first. <<Q2>>
D: How do you see your lungs at this point? What have the other doctors told you?
P: They said the lungs are worn out. It won't get better, only slower or faster. <<Q4>>
D: When decisions come up, who should we talk with?
P: Mary decides with me. Kevin too, he handles the paperwork. <<Q3>>
D: Is there something that scares you when you think ahead?
P: Choking. Not being able to get air at night. <<Q5>>
F: He wakes up in a panic sometimes.
D: We can give medicine for that breathlessness at night. We will start a low dose and see you in a week. <<Q7>>
P: Alright. <<Q7>>
F: Okay. I'll bring him.
