# Sahara symptom tracker: clinical design review

Research checked: 23 October 2026. Prepared for the clinical team, Shirin and Sujay. **Working design brief, not an approved clinical protocol or a claim that Sahara is clinically validated.** No patient information was used. No instrument registration, licence purchase or external message was made. The subsequent bounded web changes and their actual checks are recorded below.

## The decision to make

Use **one clinician-selected, versioned instrument** for a formal assessment, with a short personal diary alongside it. Do not combine convenient questions from different scales and call the result ESAS-r, IPOS, a clinical distress score or triage.

For discussion: **ESAS-r** is the simpler repeated symptom-severity candidate; **Hindi/English IPOS** is the stronger candidate for a broader palliative review covering physical, emotional, practical and communication concerns. A longer clinical review does not have to run every day. The team should choose the instrument, recall period, frequency and who actually reads the results before implementation.

### VAS is not another name for a 0–10 picker

A numerical rating scale offers discrete numbers, usually eleven choices from 0 to 10. A traditional **visual analogue scale (VAS)** is a continuous line, commonly 100 mm, with endpoint anchors; the distance of the person's mark is measured. A row of 0–10 buttons should be called an NRS, not a VAS. Do not convert either to the other merely by changing its label. NCI describes both and distinguishes symptom intensity from interference with daily activity. [NCI pain assessment](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/pain/pain-hp-pdq)

### Symptoms and language to review

The standard ESAS-r domains are pain, tiredness, drowsiness, nausea, lack of appetite, shortness of breath, depression/sadness, anxiety/nervousness, and overall wellbeing, plus an optional other problem. Tiredness and sleepiness must remain distinct; wellbeing and appetite require particular care with the direction of their anchors. [AHS tool overview](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/info/page14546.aspx)

For an optional personal diary, the clinicians can additionally consider constipation, diarrhoea, vomiting, sleep difficulty, sore/dry mouth, mobility or wound/tube concerns **when relevant to the person**. This is a review list, not an instruction to ask everyone every question or to add these to a validated score. The Ontario extended form and the Hindi IPOS study support considering concerns beyond pain alone. [Ontario extended form](https://www.cancercareontario.ca/sites/ccocancercare/files/assets/CCOESAS-r-Plus.pdf), [Hindi IPOS study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0006189)

Use existing authorised Hindi versions rather than translating instrument wording through an AI model. An official **Hindi ESAS-r v1:2015** is publicly posted by Ontario Health. Its PDF text extraction contains broken glyphs; do not paste that extraction into the app. The original visual wording still needs inspection before use. Availability of a translation alone does not establish its measurement properties in our target population. [Official Hindi form](https://www.cancercareontario.ca/sites/ccocancercare/files/assets/CCOESAS-Hindi.pdf)

### Current Hindi evidence worth taking to the team

Bhardwaj and colleagues published a Hindi IPOS validation on **3 April 2026**, involving **240 adults receiving CanSupport home-based cancer palliative care in Delhi NCR**. The study used trained interviewer administration, with follow-ups at approximately four-to-six-week intervals; it did not test Sahara, daily self-entry or speech recognition. Physical/emotional domains performed better than the communication/quality-of-care domain, whose internal consistency was low (alpha 0.35). Floor effects, regional recruitment, exclusions of people too ill/unable to speak, and preliminary responsiveness estimates limit extrapolation. This is good reason to evaluate the approved Hindi IPOS, not permission to promise nationwide or app-specific validity. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0006189)

A 2023 electronic ESAS-r study tested wording and remote completion with 26 patient/family advisors in Alberta. The authors expressly allow their exact electronic questionnaire to be built into digital platforms, but tested it only in English and identified limited representativeness. It supports cognitive usability testing; it does not validate a newly translated or shortened Sahara version. [Primary study and electronic tool](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20552076231190998)

The Hindi IPOS paper links a public supplementary document containing the full **patient, past-week Hindi form**, marked `IPOSv1-P7-IN-29/07/2019`. Its embedded two-page image was visually inspected: symptoms use 0–4, other question anchors differ, and it records who helped complete it. The public research supplement was downloaded directly from the publisher; the separate POS account-gated download route was not used. This copy does not waive the instrument owner's usage terms. [Public supplement](https://journals.plos.org/globalpublichealth/article/file?type=supplementary&id=10.1371/journal.pgph.0006189.s001)

## Proposed short journey for clinical review

These are original product-design proposals, separate from the wording/scoring of any named instrument.

1. **Open the person's check-in.** Keep the selected person and language. Remember whether the last entry was self-reported or assisted, but ask for confirmation when the respondent changes. Never silently turn a caregiver observation into the patient's own answer.
2. **Show the current question and its time frame.** For example, a chosen ESAS-r version retains its exact now instruction. An informal diary might ask only what is bothering the person, but must not be labelled a completed ESAS-r.
3. **Make choices visible.** Large 0–10 buttons with both endpoint meanings, no default score, no required free typing. Offer an explicit unable/not answered route. Missing is not zero. Do not hide routine choices behind dropdowns.
4. **Ask context only when useful.** For pain, optional location and whether it occurs at rest, while turning in bed, standing/walking, coughing, or another situation. These are descriptions of what already happened; do not ask someone to perform a painful movement to obtain a score. Provide not sure and an optional short note. A pain trigger is not an automatic diagnosis of mechanical pain or a fracture score.
5. **Save and show the actual entry.** Show the original responses, date/time, respondent and any context. A local save is not proof that a clinician received or reviewed it. Keep a separate team receipt/review state if that workflow exists.

Generic diary navigation copy for Hindi review (original draft, **not** substitute wording for ESAS-r or IPOS):

| English | Hindi draft |
| --- | --- |
| What is bothering you most? | सबसे ज़्यादा क्या परेशानी है? |
| Compared with your last note on [date] | [तारीख] के पिछले नोट के मुकाबले |
| Better / Same / Worse / Not sure | बेहतर / वैसा ही / ज़्यादा खराब / पक्का नहीं |
| Written by the patient / With help / Caregiver's observation | मरीज़ ने बताया / मदद से लिखा / देखभाल करने वाले ने देखा |

Have Hindi-speaking patients and clinicians review these distinctions; a caregiver entering an answer on the patient's behalf is different from independently observing a symptom.

### Baseline, yesterday and change

- Baseline means a designated **real recorded assessment**, not a seeded comparison value. Label its date and instrument. If no prior matching entry exists, say first entry and omit a change result.
- After a patient has recorded today's response, show a nearby actual previous entry using the same symptom, scale, recall and respondent context. Do not prefill the new severity from yesterday or show it before answering by default; that can anchor a fresh response.
- A raw change may be shown factually: `4/10 now; 6/10 at yesterday's 18:10 check-in`. Avoid percentages, overall recovery claims and an invented clinically important difference. If the previous entry is three days old, say so rather than calling it yesterday.
- A separate **better / same / worse / not sure** comparison can capture the person's own view, with an explicit reference date. This is a proposed diary question, not a new validated scale. It may disagree with the number and should be retained rather than overwritten.
- Keep now, worst in 24 hours, average in 24 hours, at-rest and movement-related ratings in different fields/series. An improvement after medicine is a temporal observation, not proof that medicine caused it.
- Do not aggregate a partial diary into a clinical distress total. If a formal instrument is adopted, follow its exact missing-item and scoring instructions; do not infer a zero or invent a replacement score.

## Distress and breathlessness options

**Distress is broader than sadness.** The NCCN Distress Thermometer is a separate screen, commonly anchored over the previous week with a problem list; it is not a diagnosis of depression. A 2026 NCCN guideline update exists, but its current full tool and reproduction terms were not accessible in this run. Do not copy an older diagram or deploy a cut-off from a search result. If the team chooses it, obtain the current official form and app-distribution permission terms first. [2026 NCCN primary guideline record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41956112/), [NCCN-authored older full guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6907687/)

**Breathlessness intensity and activity limitation are different.** The clinician can use a consistent symptom NRS within the chosen instrument. Original MRC grades the effect on activity on a 1–5 scale; modified mMRC is commonly 0–4. Neither should be substituted for a symptom 0–10 score, and neither is oxygen saturation. UKRI grants reuse of the original MRC with credit, but cannot authorise modified versions or translations. [Official MRC resource](https://www.ukri.org/councils/mrc/facilities-and-resources/find-an-mrc-facility-or-resource/mrc-dyspnoea-scale/)

**Borg CR10 is not a generic NRS.** It uses its own descriptors/instructions and is copyrighted. The owner directs software/system providers to a specific licensing route; even its free academic licence requires agreement and prohibits modifying wording or adding colours. Do not add a Borg-labelled scale simply because both interfaces contain a 10. [Owner's licensing categories](https://borgperception.se/obtain-a-license/), [academic terms](https://borgperception.se/obtain-a-license-for-research-and-education/)

This brief intentionally does **not** specify medication doses, treatment actions, traffic-light risk bands or an automated emergency rule. A care-team contact action needs a real verified contact and an agreed review/response process. A symptom score must not promise continuous monitoring or reassure someone that waiting is safe.

## Rights and implementation gates

| Source/instrument | What is established | Before app use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ESAS-r | AHS manual says free use with acknowledgement; changing the current form/name requires author consent | Select exact version/translation, preserve attribution, and resolve any intended wording/layout changes with the owners. Do not claim author approval from a download |
| Electronic ESAS-r (2023 study) | Authors expressly permit digital implementation of their tested form | Keep its provenance distinct; clarify changes and Hindi adaptation rather than applying the English evidence to new wording |
| IPOS | Owner grants free use, disallows charging for the measure, requires registration/terms for people to whom it is supplied, attribution and permission for wording changes | Review app-distribution/registration arrangement with the owners; use the approved Hindi/English files. Added questions remain outside the instrument. [Owner's terms](https://pos-pal.org/maix/terms-and-conditions.php) |
| BPI | Copyright covers derivatives/translations; owner permission is required and fees can apply | Use the authorised Hindi/English version only after applicable permission. [Owner's user guide](https://www.mdanderson.org/documents/Departments-and-Divisions/Symptom-Research/BPI_UserGuide.pdf) |
| NCCN / Borg / modified breathlessness tools | A screenshot or paper citation is not an app-distribution licence | Verify the specific current instrument and rights before inclusion |

An open-access study licence does not automatically replace a questionnaire's underlying copyright or registration requirements. No new legal terms were accepted here.

## Source gaps observed before the bounded web fix

Initial read-only source review on 23 October identified the following. These observations describe that snapshot; the bounded changes below supersede the listed implementation details where stated. No clinical test was performed.

- `prototype/app/family-symptom-diary.tsx` already supports visible, individually selected 0–10 ratings, optional onset/notes, edit/undo/export and Hindi labels. That is a useful personal diary, not automatically a validated instrument.
- `prototype/app/family-symptom-state.ts` has symptom, severity, day, onset, note and author, but no instrument/version, recall, measured time, patient-versus-proxy response, activity context or formally designated baseline. Existing legacy Mild/Moderate/Severe responses should be retained, not retroconverted to numbers.
- `prototype/app/esas-symptom-tracker.tsx` initializes patient scores **and baseline scores with fixed numbers**, sums a distress total and uses a score threshold. Those values are not measured patient/baseline evidence. Do not carry them into a real patient assessment.
- `prototype/app/breathlessness-crisis-engine.tsx` calls a 0–10 response VAS. That terminology should be reviewed against its actual control. This research does not approve that separate clinical decision-support route, its scores or its treatment content.

### Bounded web changes completed on 23 October

The personal diary now records a chosen respondent (patient, assisted patient, caregiver observation or care-team observation), actual observation date/time, explicit now/average-24-hour/worst-24-hour context, and optional pain location/activity. No rating is selected by default. An explicit unable-to-rate response is stored as null, never zero. The first earlier comparable real entry supplies the baseline; the latest earlier matching entry supplies the dated comparison. Matching requires the same patient, symptom, scale, recall, respondent and pain context; independent proxy observers must also match. Legacy records without that context remain readable but are not silently compared. Better/same/worse/not-sure remains a separate attributed response attached to its actual reference entry.

The former ESAS-labelled panel now opens these generic personal notes through the existing caller's store. Fabricated scores/baseline, clinical distress total, automatic severity colours, threshold-based reassurance and medication instructions were removed from that panel. It does not claim to administer a validated ESAS-r or IPOS; formal instrument selection remains for clinical review. Other clinical decision-support routes were not changed.

Ten focused state tests pass, covering missing and zero ratings, first/actual baseline, incompatible contexts, proxy identity, independent patient change, edit clearing and persisted JSON round-trip/export. TypeScript passed during this change; the integrated project/browser/deployment checks belong to the main task. A JSON round trip is not proof of browser storage receipt or clinical delivery, and no patient pilot or app validation is claimed.

## Decisions for Shirin, Sujay and the clinical team

1. Is the intended user a communicative adult with cancer, a caregiver helping that adult, or someone unable to self-report? Choose the respondent policy and clinician route for the last group.
2. Choose **formal instrument + exact Hindi/English version + recall**. Is routine review symptom-focused (ESAS-r), holistic (IPOS), or a personal diary with no instrument claim?
3. Agree the minimum assessment schedule, actual review owner, response hours, escalation contact and what happens when no clinician is assigned. Avoid collecting daily scores nobody will read.
4. Review separate tiredness/sleepiness and appetite/wellbeing anchors with Hindi-speaking users; confirm approved wording and read-aloud support. Specify pain location/trigger questions and when they are optional.
5. Decide which raw comparisons are useful, which constitute an actionable change, and who interprets them. Do not derive automatic triage/treatment rules from this brief.
6. Before a patient pilot, test understanding, accessibility, missing answers, patient switching, offline/retry, actual save/receipt, corrections and permissions with fictional entries; then follow the clinical partner's consent, privacy and oversight process.
