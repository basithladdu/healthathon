# Palliative care providers in India (beyond Pallium, Niram, Arike, IAPC, Kerala)

_Research agent report, 27 Oct 2026. Unedited except for this header._

## Palliative care providers in India other than Pallium, Niram, Arike, IAPC and Kerala: research for Sahara (27 Oct 2026)

**Method and limits.** I first checked what the repo already has. It already records Cipla's Saath-Saath name clash (PRODUCT_NAMING.md), PalliKare (CARE_DEEP_DIVE) and the Hindi IPOS validation on CanSupport patients (symptom-tracker brief). This report adds what is new beyond those.

WebFetch failed with DNS errors on most Indian sites, so I used WebSearch plus curl with its normal user agent. Two sites could not be read at all:
- **cansupport.org** returned a Mod_Security "Not Acceptable" block. I did not try to get around it, so CanSupport figures come from third parties.
- **tmc.gov.in** could not be fetched; its figures come from search excerpts.

I did not read Play Store or App Store pages directly. Directory phone numbers (APHN, IAHPC, IAPC) are **[unverified]**, because the directories say they don't verify them. Everything was read-only.

### (1) Key findings for Sahara

1. **None of the providers in my scope offers a family-facing app.** What exists:
   - PalliKare is a reference app for clinicians only.
   - AIIMS Delhi ran an ICMR randomised trial of a *patient* medication-adherence app. The app had "reminder alarms, drug information, symptoms scoring" (palliativecare.in, Feb 2022). It won the National e-Governance Silver Award 2020-21, and I found no public product from it.
   - Tata Memorial and AIIMS use phone and WhatsApp telemedicine. Tata Memorial had 314 patients during the 2020 lockdown (IJPC 2020); AIIMS ran a "smartphone-based 24x7" model.
   - The usual digital channel is a phone line. Saath-Saath is toll-free on 1800-202-7777, Monday to Saturday, 10am to 6pm. It has 36 partner organisations and "over 15,000 callers" since 2021 (The Logical Indian, 2026).
2. **CanSupport is the single best Hindi-belt fit.**
   - It has had 28–29 free home-care teams in Delhi, Haryana, UP, Punjab and Jharkhand.
   - Patients under home care at any time: 2,142 to 2,642 a year from 2017-18 to 2021-22. Home visits: 36,389 in 2021-22 (give.do).
   - It has its own helpline, OPD clinics near Delhi's main cancer hospitals, and bereavement counselling for up to 6 months (2009–10 data).
   - The 2026 Hindi IPOS was validated on CanSupport patients, so a Sahara Hindi symptom diary has a ready clinical anchor.
3. **AIIMS New Delhi has the strongest research credentials in the north.**
   - Its Onco-Anaesthesia & Palliative Medicine department is a WHO Collaborating Centre for training and education in palliative care.
   - It co-runs an annual foundation course with the CanSupport home-care team.
   - It already ran a mobile-app trial, so it could validate Sahara. It could equally see Sahara as overlapping its own work.
4. **Emmanuel Hospital Association (EHA) is the only rural Hindi-heartland home-care network I found.**
   - It began at Harriet Benson Memorial Hospital, Lalitpur (UP) in 2009.
   - It has "seven rural hospitals and one community team in Delhi". Each team carries "30-40 patients at any one time", visits "3-5 patients a day" and keeps "24/7 on call" (ehospice).
   - A third-party listing says it works across 9 states.
   - It is a good test of Sahara for low-literacy users and patchy connectivity.
5. **Karunashraya (Bangalore Hospice Trust) has the best family-education content, but none in Hindi.**
   - Its "Moments That Matter" caregiver animations launched in English on 14 Feb 2026 and in Kannada on 1 April 2026. "Odia and additional regional languages" are next. Hindi is not named.
   - It also co-launched the Karunashraya–MAHE Centre of Excellence for Advance Care Planning on 7 Oct 2025.
   - Both are direct partnership hooks: Hindi content in Sahara, and help validating Sahara's goals-of-care flow.
6. **Cipla Foundation is the ecosystem funder and convener.** It supports over 65,900 people a year, runs the Pune centre (26,000+ patients since 1997, 50 beds as of 2016) and runs Saath-Saath. It also funded Sukoon Nilaya and the B.J. Wadia paediatric unit. It is a likely CSR funder or distribution partner. Avoid "Saath" in naming (already noted in the repo).
7. **There is a gap in non-cancer and neuro-palliative care, and it is growing.**
   - Sukoon Nilaya in Mumbai has grown from 12 to 35 free beds for non-cancer patients. It "trains caregivers… follow-up through phone and video consultations" (Oct 2026).
   - IHBAS Delhi opened a Neuropalliative OPD on 1 Nov 2023 for dementia, stroke and ALS, covering patients and caregivers.
   - Sparsh Hospice in Hyderabad opened an 8-bed neuro-palliative ward in July 2026.
   - Long-duration families in these settings would use a diary and medicines app the most.
8. **Hindi-belt coverage is thin and mostly hospital-based.**
   - **Uttar Pradesh:** in 2021 KGMU said "the concept of a hospice is yet to start in Uttar Pradesh". SGPGIMS has run home visits since 2010.
   - **Bihar:** OPD and inpatient services at Mahavir Cancer Sansthan, Anjani Anurag Hospice, AIIMS Patna and HBCH Muzaffarpur. Home-care teams for 5 districts were still only a proposal in Sept 2024.
   - **Madhya Pradesh:** no state palliative care policy as of 2023.
   - **Rajasthan:** Bhagwan Mahaveer Cancer Hospital, Jaipur (home care since 1998) and AIIMS Jodhpur (OPD since 2018, shared care with Barmer district hospital).
   - Families in this belt travel far and get few home visits. Remote diary and visit logging is where Sahara adds the most value.
9. **The typical home-care pattern is an in-person team plus a phone line plus counselling.**
   - Tata Memorial: home care since 1997–98, about 1,100 families a year, 3,000+ home-care patients a year according to a 2021 paper.
   - Cipla Pune: about 200 visits a month (2016).
   - Ganga Prem Hospice, Rishikesh: 3 teams, 391 home patients in 2021-22.
   - Between visits these teams have no structured record of what the family sees. That is Sahara's slot.
10. **Telangana is the clearest model of state-plus-NGO palliative care,** but its languages (Telugu and Urdu) fall outside Sahara's Hindi/English focus.
    - The Pain Relief & Palliative Care Society (Dr Gayatri Palat) is linked to MNJ Institute of Oncology. The state has palliative centres in all 33 districts with home care: 16,822 home visits from April 2018 to June 2019.
    - Two Worlds Cancer Collaboration is a Canadian funder of this work (MNJ, Kurnool, Mandara Children's Hospice). It also uses ECHO tele-mentoring, which suggests openness to digital tools.
    - It is a second-wave option for a Hyderabad Hindi/Urdu/English pilot.

**Top 5 pilot partners for a Hindi/English family app**
1. **CanSupport (Delhi NCR).** Largest Hindi home-care caseload, a validated Hindi IPOS, its own helpline, free care for the poor, and no app. Risk: it is a small NGO with limited IT capacity, and its site blocks automated reading. Approach through Saath-Saath or the AIIMS training link.
2. **AIIMS New Delhi Palliative Medicine.** Gives clinical validation and an evaluation partner (WHO Collaborating Centre, ICMR app trial, 24x7 telemedicine). Risk: ethics approval timelines, and its own in-house app work.
3. **Emmanuel Hospital Association (rural UP and the north).** A true Hindi-heartland home model with small caseloads. Risk: low smartphone ownership and literacy, so voice and offline features are required.
4. **Cipla Foundation / Saath-Saath.** Funding plus a national referral channel (36 partners, 15,000+ callers). Sukoon Nilaya, which it funded, fits as a Mumbai Hindi site focused on caregiver training. Risk: the name clash with "Saath", and possible conflict of interest because Cipla sells medicines.
5. **Ganga Prem Hospice, Rishikesh.** Hindi, 24x7, free, small (about 400 home patients a year), quick to start. Alternates: HBCH/MPMMCC Varanasi (Tata Memorial in UP), BMCHRC Jaipur, PGIMER Chandigarh. Karunashraya is a content partner rather than a pilot site.

### (3) Gaps nobody fills that Sahara could
- **Between-visit structured family reporting in Hindi.** Every provider relies on visits, phone calls or WhatsApp, and none uses a family diary tied to the Hindi IPOS.
- **Hindi caregiver skills content.** Karunashraya's animations exist only in English and Kannada, and Saath-Saath gives referrals, not ongoing support.
- **Follow-up after a helpline call or discharge.** Saath-Saath refers callers onward. Sukoon Nilaya and Sparsh discharge to home with phone check-ins. There is no shared medicines and missed-dose record.
- **Families in the Hindi belt with no home team.** Bihar, much of UP and Madhya Pradesh have hospital OPD only.
- **Long non-cancer journeys** (dementia, ALS, stroke): IHBAS, Sukoon Nilaya, the Sparsh neuro ward, the Cipla–NIMHANS unit.
- **A family-confirmed advance care planning record in Hindi.** The new Karunashraya–MAHE centre is aimed at clinicians and institutions.
- **Bereavement follow-up.** CanSupport offers up to 6 months but has no digital continuity.

### (4) Threats, competitors and what not to claim
- AIIMS's ICMR medication-adherence app (reminders, symptom scoring) is clinical prior art. Don't claim Sahara is "the first app for palliative medicine adherence in India".
- PalliKare (Karunashraya) is aimed at clinicians, so it is not a direct competitor. Saath-Saath is a phone line, not a competitor, but it owns the "Saath" brand.
- WhatsApp is the real competitor: Tata Memorial published on it in 2015, and teams use it informally.
- The R21 India Pal-Care trial (Tata Medical Center Kolkata, community health workers, NCT04972630) may produce a competing home-care model.
- Don't claim any of these organisations as partners, users or endorsers. Don't quote CanSupport's "24,000 a year" (2017) next to "2,200 at a time" without saying they measure different things.
- Don't claim the Hindi IPOS is Sahara's validated instrument. It was validated with trained interviewers, not as a family self-report app.
- Bed and patient counts conflict between sources (Shanti Avedna, Sparsh, Tata Memorial). Cite them with years.
- Don't publish directory phone numbers as verified.
- Don't treat the morphine and opioid workflows these clinics run as something Sahara manages. Sahara records whether doses were taken or missed; it does not prescribe.
