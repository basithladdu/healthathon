### (1) Key findings for Sahara

1. **Consumer apps in this category don't pay for themselves.**
   - CaringBridge, the largest family-update platform, runs on about $10–11M a year of individual donations. Give.org: contributions "~$10.3M", total income "$11,115,522", expenses "~$12.0M" (2024).
   - Jointly (Carers UK) charges a one-off "£2.99 per circle". Councils and employers buy free access in bulk for residents and staff (West Berkshire code "WESTBERKS").
   - Lotsa Helping Hands earns from fees paid by hospice and health organisations, plus sponsorships. Non-profit partners pay nothing.
   - For Sahara, this means families should never be the main payer.
2. **The oncology ePRO tools that became sellable products sold to hospitals and were bought by device makers.**
   - Noona was bought by Varian in October 2018 (terms not disclosed).
   - Kaiku Health was bought by Elekta in May 2020; it was "used in over 40 European cancer clinics".
   - Carevive was bought by Health Catalyst in June 2024, after about $29–40M raised.
   - Canopy raised $13M (2022) selling to oncology practices.
   - Families did not pay for any of them; clinics paid, inside the clinical workflow.
3. **Evidence is mixed, and Sahara must not quote it as its own.**
   - STAR (Basch, JAMA 2017; single centre, 766 patients) found median survival of 31.2 vs 26.0 months.
   - The larger PRO-TECT trial (52 practices, 1,191 patients; Nature Medicine 2025) found no survival difference: "HR 0.99 (0.83–1.17); P = 0.86".
   - PRO-TECT did find fewer emergency visits (HR 0.84) and slower decline in physical function, symptoms and quality of life. Adherence was 91.5%.
   - These were patient self-report trials with clinician alerts. None was a family app.
4. **Goals-of-care evidence is real but modest.**
   - PREPARE (JAMA Intern Med 2018, 986 patients): documented advance care planning rose to "43.0% vs 32.0%". It worked equally well in English and Spanish and across literacy levels.
   - The Serious Illness Care Program trial (JAMA Intern Med 2019) improved patients' mental-health symptoms. It "was unable to demonstrate" that care matched patients' goals, and it did not change survival.
5. **Caregiver coordination apps have almost no outcome evidence.**
   - A 2024 JMIR scoping review found 53% pilots and 38% qualitative studies, with only 2 experimental studies. "None of the identified studies assessed an intervention of a caregiver app for care planning and coordination."
   - CaringBridge has only qualitative studies and its own surveys.
   - An Indian pragmatic trial would be both a real gap Sahara could fill and its best fundraising asset.
6. **The closest Indian analogues are non-profits that run through government systems, not startups.**
   - Noora Health (family-caregiver training in hospitals) uses "public partnership agreements" that build the programme into "government health budgets". It is funded by Audacious/TED (2022) and Skoll.
   - ARMMAN's Kilkari is a "co-invested public-private partnership" with the Health Ministry, backed by Gates, MSD for Mothers, Co-Impact and CSR (Cognizant Foundation).
   - Khushi Baby is "Nodal Technical Service Provider" to Rajasthan's health department.
7. **Indian Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) money for palliative care is real and already flowing.**
   - CSR is the legally required company spending under Schedule VII of the Companies Act; item (i) covers "promoting health care including preventive health care". Total CSR was ₹34,908.75 cr in FY24, of which healthcare was ₹7,150.81 cr.
   - Optum's FY26 CSR plan funds CanSupport's palliative programme under item (i).
   - Cipla Foundation partnerships reached "more than 65,900 patients" in FY26. Cipla's CSR budget for FY26 is ₹103.75 cr.
   - HCLTech Grant: Pallium India was runner-up in Health in 2025 and won ₹25 lakh. Winners get ₹5 cr.
   - The catch: CSR money must reach a registered implementing agency. That means a Section 8 company, trust or society with 12A/80G tax registration and a CSR-1 filing, and a three-year track record unless the company set it up. A for-profit Sahara can only be a paid vendor to such an agency.
8. **Government money exists but is slow.**
   - The National Programme for Palliative Care (NPPC) has "no separate budget". It sits in the NHM "Mission Flexipool", so states must ask for it in their annual plans (PIPs).
   - NPPC covers 600 districts (to October 2025). In 2025-26 to 25 October: over 19 lakh home visits, 12.6 lakh outpatient visits and 3.3 lakh inpatient admissions.
   - Palliative care is one of the 12 service packages at the 1,78,368 Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (primary health centres).
   - Kerala's model, where panchayats pay, is under strain. In Kannur (July 2026), "salaries… pending for the last six months".
9. **Open source and Digital Public Good (DPG) status is already held by an adjacent player.**
   - OHC's CARE EMR is recognised as "the 50th Digital Public Good" and is used in Kerala home palliative care (OHC self-reports about 273,000 people) [unverified].
   - The DPG Standard has 9 indicators, including an approved open licence, privacy, Sustainable Development Goal relevance and do-no-harm.
   - A family-side module that works alongside CARE is a stronger story than a competing platform.
10. **Shutdowns and distressed sales are common, and family out-of-pocket strain is severe.**
    - Aetna shut its InvolveCare caregiver app before launch (2014).
    - CareZone sold its app technology to Walmart (2020, about $200M reported).
    - Cake (end-of-life planning) was bought by a funeral chain in 2024 after about $5M raised.
    - Karkinos (Tata-backed cancer care) went through insolvency and was sold to Reliance for ₹375 cr, "around half its earlier valuation" (2024).
    - Indian healthtech funding fell 55% in 2022.
    - One in five households of older adults needing palliative care faces catastrophic health spending. Families liquidate savings (24.4%) or take loans (26.5%).

**C. Recommended sustainability paths for Sahara**

1. **CSR-funded deployment through a palliative NGO (main path, 12–24 months).**
   - Partner with a palliative provider that already receives CSR, such as CanSupport, Cipla Palliative Care Centre Pune, Pallium India, or a Saath-Saath consortium member.
   - The NGO is the CSR implementing agency. Sahara is either its technology vendor or set up as a Section 8 company. CSR-1 rules mean a new Section 8 entity can't receive CSR as an implementing agency without a three-year record unless the company sets it up.
   - Pitch it as cost per family supported, with the NGO's team reviewing goals-of-care notes.
   - Targets: Cipla Foundation, Optum, HCLTech Grant (₹5 cr winner tier), Tata Trusts cancer programme.
2. **Open source / DPG plus a state NHM home-care integration (credibility and scale, 2–4 years).**
   - Open-license the code and build toward the DPG Standard.
   - Position Sahara as the family-side companion to OHC CARE or to state NPPC home-based teams; ASHA and nurse visits already happen at scale.
   - Approach one state's NHM to add it to its plan (PIP), copying Noora, ARMMAN and Khushi Baby: free to government at first, co-invested later.
   - Expect delays: Kerala's own funding is stalled.
3. **Grant-funded pragmatic evaluation as a bridge (6–18 months).**
   - Use BIRAC BIG, Pfizer INDovation or a Tata Trusts grant for a controlled study.
   - Measure goals-of-care documentation, family confirmation, missed medicines and caregiver burden at one or two NPPC or NGO sites.
   - No Indian family-app outcome data exists; PREPARE and Kaiku-style evidence is what made comparators fundable and saleable.

Paths not recommended now: consumer subscriptions, insurer deals, and selling data.

### (3) Gaps nobody fills that Sahara could

- No family-side palliative app in India that combines a symptom and medicine diary with a doctor-reviewed goals-of-care note the family confirms. pal (Netherlands) is the nearest analogue globally.
- No Hindi or Indian cultural adaptation of SICG or PREPARE was found. Only Punjabi copies (Stanford, Vancouver), a Singapore study and a South Asian-Canadian focus group exist.
- No outcome trial of a caregiver coordination app found anywhere (2024 scoping review).
- OHC CARE and NPPC tools are clinician-side. Families have no channel into NPPC home-care teams.
- Cost-of-care and paperwork help for families: one in five face catastrophic spending, and no comparator handles it.

### (4) Threats, competitors and what not to claim

- **Survival:** don't claim a survival benefit. PRO-TECT was null on survival, and STAR was single-centre and patient-reported, not a family app.
- **Legal status:** don't call the goals-of-care note an advance directive or living will. The Supreme Court's 2023 modification still requires "two witnesses along with a notary or gazetted officer" and a medical board.
- **DPG and CSR labels:** don't say "DPG" before registry acceptance. Don't say "CSR-eligible" as if Sahara could receive CSR directly; only a registered implementing agency can.
- **Government money:** don't imply NPPC has a budget line; it is Flexipool, requested through state plans.
- **Competitors and partners:** OHC CARE (DPG, Kerala palliative work); Cipla Saath-Saath helpline (1800-202-7777); pal (could expand); hospital EMR vendors.
- **Business-model threats:**
  - An acquirer could repurpose the product (Cake ended up with a funeral chain).
  - Donation-driven models can annoy users (CaringBridge reviews complain about donation prompts).
  - Data-monetisation optics (Belong) under DPDP.
  - Healthtech funding winter; Karkinos distress sale.
- **Vendor figures:** Canopy, Vesta, Noona and Noora outcome numbers are self-reported. Don't quote them as established.
