# Arike: partner research report (for Sahara)

**Two different things share the name "Arike".**
- **Arike NGO**, legal name "Arike Daycare Nurses". This is a 24/7 home-care and palliative NGO in Kochi, supported by IMA Cochin. It is the CARE 2.0→3.0 deployment that OHC wrote about. Its site is **arikecare.org**. arike.care does not resolve, and the proxy returned 502.
- **"ARIKE" software (2021)**. This was a CoronaSafe Rails app, written to a spec for NHM Ernakulam's government palliative network. Its live site, arike.coronasafe.in, is dead.

They are linked through people (Dr. Athul Joseph Manuel) and place (Ernakulam, the General Hospital palliative team). I found nothing saying the NGO used the 2021 app. [unverified]

## 1. Who they are

**Model**
- "Arike, supported by IMA Cochin, is a demonstration centre for home care. In Kochi, our doctors, nurses, physiotherapists, and social workers care for patients at home, around the clock. What we learn becomes research, training, and support for the institutions…" (https://arikecare.org/)
- Four pillars (https://arikecare.org/):
  - "You stay home"
  - "A single multidisciplinary team… coordinate as one team through a 24/7 control room"
  - "Care, never denied for cost. A free care model: families who can, contribute voluntarily; those who cannot are supported through CSR and community donations"
  - "Community as the unit of care"
- In the IJPC 2025 poster: "hub and spoke model to collaborate with mid-level hospitals to create dedicated palliative care units… Cross-Subsidisation… monthly social audits involve volunteers and staff" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12362036/ ; Indian J Palliat Care 2025;31:119-53, doi 10.25259/IJPC_118_2025).

(https://palliumindia.org/2019/08/by-your-side-arike-a-unique-palliative-care-service). "Arike" = "By your side".

## 2. How they work day to day

**Control room and helpline**
- "A round-the-clock control room handling 33,132 calls and 11,262 teleconsultations a year" (https://arikecare.org/services).
- Day vs night (AR25):

| | Day | Night |
|---|---|---|
| All calls | 17,267 | 15,865 |
| Doctor tele-consultations | 5,253 | 1,992 |
| Nurse tele-consultations | 3,335 | 682 |

- AR25: "Night-time communication demand remained consistently high, highlighting the importance of round-the-clock responsiveness in managing pain crises, symptom escalation, and caregiver distress."
- External telemedicine: "44 cases with government primary teams, 56 with secondary care, and 40 NGO-supported interactions."

**Home visits**
- Doctor visits: "pain, infections, breathlessness… regular follow-up to monitor disease progression and refine care plans… Guidance for caregivers".
- Nursing visits: "Wound care… Catheter, stoma, and infusion care… Administration of medications and injections… Monitoring of vital signs" (AR25).
- Night care: "coordinated model involving the control room, on-call doctors, and nursing staff" (AR25).
- Fleet: 4 EV scooters and 5 EV cars; "Integration of vehicle tracking and planned routing" (AR25).

**OPD**
- No OPD is described. Patients are referred to Sudheendra Hospital "for terminal patients experiencing sudden symptom flare-ups who require temporary inpatient supportive care" (AR25).

**Psychosocial and bereavement**
- "Each patient undergoes a structured psychosocial assessment to identify emotional distress, caregiver strain, financial vulnerability…"
- Bereavement: "Follow-up home visits and counselling" — "182 families supported in 2025" (site); AR25's table says 184.
- Psychiatric home care: 71 (with MEHAC). Dementia screening: 19 (AR25).

**Volunteers (KOODE)**
- Tiered roles: "companions, home helpers, peer educators, and community coordinators… monthly reflection circles".
- Volunteers act as "A bridge to the team… Keeping families and the clinical team connected, so care stays continuous and timely" (https://arikecare.org/programmes).
- UNARV youth workshops ran across 19 colleges.

**Training (https://arikecare.org/programmes)**
- Foundation Course in Home Medicine (doctors, 10 days)
- CCCPN (nurses, 6 months)
- 120-day Certificate Course in Home Care Nursing
- Ostomy-care training
- VCPC volunteer course (3 batches a year)
- Monthly 10-day course for "new volunteers & caregivers"
- 2025: 50 social-work interns and 12 international nurses.

**Caregiver support**
- Poster: "Regular sensitisation programs, volunteer training and basic nursing skills to empower caregivers."
- Oct 2024 EoL workshop: "Emphasis was also placed on empowering caregivers with effective communication strategies and self-care" (https://www.palliativecare.in/testimonial/workshop-on-end-of-life-care-at-kochi/).

**Equipment lending, 2025**
- 41 air beds, 40 hospital beds, 32 oxygen concentrators, 19 wheelchairs, 9 suction machines, 1 BiPAP, among others.

**Medicines and opioids**
- No public statement on morphine, RMI status or opioid access was found anywhere ("morphine" and "opioid" appear 0 times on the site and in AR25).
- They mention only "Assistance with medicines" and that CSR "enabled the procurement of essential medicines". **This is a gap to ask about.**

**Software history: the 2021 "ARIKE" app** (repo https://github.com/coronasafe/arike, "Central software system Palliative Care", MIT, © 2021 CoronaSafe Network; I cloned it read-only into the scratchpad)
- About 900 commits, Mar–Dec 2021; most activity was in Mar–Apr 2021; last commit 12 Dec 2021. Started by Bodhish Thomas.
- Stack: Rails + ReScript, Devise and Pundit. UUIDs "so records can be saved on a mobile device without internet" (README).
- Roles: Superuser, Medical Officer, Primary Nurse, Secondary Nurse, ASHA, Volunteer. ASHA and Volunteer could self-sign-up.
- Data model includes:
  - LSG body, ward, facility
  - `family_details` (name, phone, relation, education, occupation)
  - `physical_symptoms` (pain, shortness of breath, nausea, constipation, sleep…)
  - `psychological_reviews` ("patient_worried", "family_anxious", "patient_informed")
  - `patient_views` / `family_views` on the patient record — an early goals-of-care-like field
  - visits with next/expected visit.
- Spec (https://model.coronasafe.network/centralised-facilities/palliative_homecare): "NHM wants to create a Central software system… prioritising Palliative Care/Homecare as a subset."
- Problems it listed:
  - "Planning for a visit in itself takes up 1-2 days"
  - "Specialist nurse needs to be informed over the phone… time delayed"
  - "There are 18,000 such patients in Ernakulam currently"
- It planned a patient-side view: "**Patients need to have access to their basic case sheet - using a QR code/mobile phone authentication**".
- It names "Dr Athul" as the source for the ward-to-block mapping.
- This lineage evidently became CARE's palliative module. [unverified]

## 4. Pain points and gaps they describe

**Hours gap**
- "most services operate only between 10am and 4pm - leaving patients without support during evenings, nights, weekends, and emergencies" (https://arikecare.org/understand).
- "Patients are often forced to seek expensive hospital care for manageable emergencies" (AR25).

**Caregivers**
- "untrained caregivers - often elderly spouses - face burnout, declining health, and financial hardship" (https://arikecare.org/understand).
- "Caregivers - often elderly spouses or women - bear the brunt of care" (AR25).
- Their trustee's study: caregivers had a "mean age 70 years, 80% female" (see section 5).

**Coverage and workforce**
- "Of Ernakulam's 34 lakh residents, only 0.7% currently receive palliative care".
- "Scarcity of trained palliative care professionals… Inadequate volunteer engagement and weak community linkages… Limited public awareness… nearly 60% of providers charge rates unaffordable… Insufficient and inconsistent funding" (AR25).

**Cost**
- "68% of health expenditure is out-of-pocket".
- Arike's own estimate: "₹13.01 Cr in direct costs saved… 244 patients × 10 days × ₹53,333/day" (AR25). This is their estimate, not an audited figure.

**Isolation and migration**
- "Migration has left many older adults living alone or with equally aged spouses" (understand page).

**Documentation and follow-up (NHM 2021 spec, Ernakulam)**
- Paper cards "can easily be ruined/lost".
- "Patient history… only available on the paper card".
- Patients arrive without their booklet, so case sheets get duplicated.

**What families struggle with (AR25)**
- Hygiene supplies: "diapers and underpads - significantly improved dignity of care and reduced caregiver stress".
- Equipment needs for "long-term bedridden patients".

**Not publicly described**
- Advance care planning or goals-of-care.
- Opioid access.
- Language problems.
- Symptom reporting between visits.
- The only end-of-life content is the 2024 workshop: "ethical considerations… role of telehealth in supporting bedridden patients."
- The night-call volume is the clearest sign of between-visit need.

## 5. Published research and data relevant to Sahara

- **Arike poster** (IJPC 2025, PMC12362036): describes the 24/7 hub-and-spoke model with cross-subsidy. Results: "Extending services to 24/7 increased patient registrations; technology improved care coordination and monitoring." No symptom tool is named. Its keywords include "CARE software, Palliative care grid, Telemedicine, Open Healthcare Network".
- **Caregiver burden study with an Arike trustee co-author** ("Annie George, Trustee, Arike Palliative Care, Kochi"; possibly Dr. Annie P.G. [unverified]):

  - 115 caregivers in Ernakulam (government and NGO units), Oct 2023–Mar 2024.
  - Tools: **Zarit Burden Interview-22 (Malayalam) and WHOQOL-BREF (Malayalam)**.
  - "moderate to severe burden was 32.1%… Caregivers of patients aged ≥73 years (aOR=7.19)… children acting as caregivers (aOR=11.09)… prolonged disease duration (aOR=3.62)."

## 6. How Sahara could help, what they would not want, and the first ask

**Strongest point against this fit**
- Arike is unusually close to OHC: its Secretary/co-founder is OHC's CMO, it co-develops CARE, it hosts IAPC Kerala's CARE, and it already plans "patient apps for reminders, resources, and symptom tracking" under CARE/Care Scribe.
- A separate app with its own server (Supabase) risks being seen as a parallel silo or a competitor.
- The pitch has to be "the family side of *your* CARE": a CARE plugin or a FHIR-shaped feed into care.arikecare.org, built with OHC, not beside it.
- Sahara today is Hindi/English. Arike's families are Malayalam-speaking; their caregiver research used Malayalam tools. **No Malayalam means no pilot.**

**Feature ↔ pain point**

| Sahara feature | Arike pain point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Symptom diary 0–10 and 3D pain body | 15,865 night calls and pain crises. A structured report before or during the call shortens triage. | AR25: "managing pain crises, symptom escalation, and caregiver distress" |
| Medicines and taken/missed log | Nurses give medicines and injections at visits but there is no between-visit adherence data. | AR25 nursing section; the AR25 "reminders" plan |
| Visits list | 12,224 visits and routing. The family sees the next visit and confirms it. | AR25 logistics; 2021 spec "Planning for a visit… 1-2 days" |
| Documents | Lost paper cards and missing booklets. | NHM 2021 spec; "QR code/mobile phone authentication" patient view |
| Family invites with edit/read roles | Migrated children abroad and elderly spouse caregivers. Lets the remote child see and help. | understand page (migration); BMJ SPC (child caregivers aOR 11) |
| Goals-of-care conversation recorded with consent, doctor-reviewed, family-confirmed | 244 intensive end-of-life patients; EoL "ethical considerations" training; the 2021 `patient_views`/`family_views` fields | AR25; IAPC workshop; ARIKE schema |
| Caregiver check-in (suggested addition: Malayalam ZBI short form or AMC-CBI 3-item) | 32% moderate-to-severe burden, caregivers' mean age 70 | PMC13422066; PMID 42694816 |
| Bereavement follow-up prompts | 182 bereaved families | services page |

**What they would likely NOT want**
- A second record outside CARE, or double data entry for nurses.
- English or Hindi only.
- Anything that adds staff workload without feeding the control room.
- Data on a non-Indian or non-ABDM-aligned server without a DPA.
- AI or recording features that compete with Care Scribe.
- Complex UIs for 70-year-old caregivers.
- Anything suggesting patients pay. Their care is free.

**Best first ask (pilot)**
- Approach through Arike's public channel (info@arikecare.org) and, given his dual role, Dr. Athul Joseph Manuel. OHC should be in the room.
- Propose a 6–8 week pilot with about 20–30 night-call-heavy, end-of-life families. Volunteers (KOODE "bridge to the team") onboard families. Malayalam UI.
- The one success measure: symptom diary entries visible to the control room (ideally inside CARE). Track change in night-call triage time and caregiver burden score.
- First questions to ask them:
  1. Which symptom forms are in their CARE 3.0 setup?
  2. Whether Care Scribe or a CARE patient app is already being built.
  3. Morphine access workflow.
  4. Data-hosting requirements.
