# Kerala government palliative care system: research for Sahara (27 Oct 2026)

All research was read-only: no forms, logins or messages. I read about 45 sources and did about 30 searches. Raw downloads and text extracts are in `(scratch path)`. Quotes from Malayalam sources are my own translations and are marked "(ML, my translation)".

**Four findings change the brief:**
1. **Kerala's government changed in May 2026.** It is now a UDF government. The health minister is **K. Muraleedharan** (NHM Kerala site: "Sri.K. Muraleedharan Minister for Health and Devaswom"; State Mission Director "Dr. Renu Raj IAS"). Almost all of the Kerala Care / grid launch material is from the LDF government (Pinarayi Vijayan, Veena George). Treat the grid's political ownership as being in transition.

## 1. Who they are

**Model of care.** Home-based, community-supported palliative care. It is run by local governments (LSGs) through primary health institutions (PHC/FHC, now branded Janakeeya Arogya Kendram), with NGOs and volunteers alongside.
- 2019 policy: "The Kerala Model places a strong emphasis on community participation and volunteerism integrated with Primary Health Care system especially through dedicated nurses under the overall leadership of local governments." (palliumindia.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/Kerala-State-Palliative-Care-Policy-2019.pdf)
- A District Medical Officer described the home team: "a team led by a trained community nurse, supported by ASHA workers, LSG members, PHC field staff, and volunteers. The Medical Officer … stays informed about each patient through the community nurse." (thesouthfirst.com, 30 Jan 2024)
- There are three tiers:
  - **Primary:** LSG/PHC home care.
  - **Secondary:** CHCs and taluk hospitals, with inpatient beds and specialist home visits, supported by NHM.
  - **Tertiary:** palliative divisions in medical colleges.
- The policy sorts every patient into a care category that changes after each visit:
  - "Category A – Need home visit by doctor and nurse and social support"
  - "Category B – Need home visit by a nurse and social support"
  - "Category C – Need only social support."

**History**
- 1993: Pain and Palliative Care Society, Kozhikode (Rajagopal, Suresh Kumar, Asoka Kumar).
- 2001: Neighbourhood Network in Palliative Care (NNPC) formed (Lancet Commission on the Value of Death, 2022).
- 2008: Asia's first state palliative care policy.
- 2008: NRHM / Arogyakeralam state palliative care project.
- 2013: palliative care made mandatory for all LSGs [per search summary of PMC3573477-era sources; not fully read].
- 2019: revised policy, approved by cabinet on 11 Dec 2019 (TNM).
- 2023: action plan ("transformed it into an actionable program in 2023", Veena George, UNI, 1 Jul 2025).
- Nov 2024: CM decides to "unify all palliative care systems" across the Health, Social Justice and LSG departments, with LSG leading (ETV Bharat/PTI, 30 Nov 2024).
- 3 Mar 2025: grid launched. 28 Jun 2025: universal project inaugurated.

- **Access:** median travel time to the nearest centre is 14 minutes in Kerala, against 118 minutes nationally. 94.6% of residents live within 30 minutes. Kerala has "44.5 percent of India's palliative care centres" (South First, 22 Nov 2025, on the ASAR/Pallium study in ecancermedicalscience).
- **Coverage:** the WHO is quoted as saying coverage reaches "nearly 60% of Kerala's population, compared with less than 2% nationally" (Outlook, 10 Aug 2026). The Lancet Commission (2022) says services "reach over 70% of those in need, compared with the national average for India of 23%."

**Funding**
- The 2019 policy's target split: "one-third of the recurring cost through state government and local governments, one-third through contributions from corporates & philanthropists, and remaining one-third by way of voluntary service."
- In practice, LSG Plan funds plus own funds plus hospital management committee (HMC) funds: "Expenses for palliative care, including the salary for nursing staff, are met from the Plan allocation by the state government, own funds of the local bodies and HMC … funds" (Onmanorama, 31 Jul 2026).
- State budget lines:
  - 2025-26: ₹5.4 crore for "expanding pain and palliative care services" [search summary].
  - 2026-27 (LDF budget, Jan 2026): "₹5 crore is allocated for Pain-Palliative-Elderly Health Care Centres"; carer allowance raised "from ₹600 to ₹1000"; Vayomithram ₹27.50 crore (South First, 30 Jan 2026). This budget was later revised by the UDF [unverified].
- NNPCs are funded by community micro-donations. Volunteers told Scroll "We always have enough."

- The new Department of Senior Citizens Welfare was created on 20 May 2026 [Wikipedia, search summary].
- Non-government anchors: IPM Kozhikode (WHO Collaborating Centre; Dr Suresh Kumar received a WHO SEARO award in Aug 2026) and Pallium India.

## 2. How they work day to day

- **Home visits by the LSG palliative nurse**, with ASHA workers and volunteers. Registers sit with the nurse: "the palliative care nurse was the custodian of all records and registers" (Kochuvilayil et al., Public Health Action 2023, PMC9983804).
- **Visit frequency is low for doctors:** "the median number of home visits from nurses and doctors in 12 months were five and one respectively" (Philip et al., BMC Palliative Care 2018, Malappuram).
- **Per provider per year (median):** 1,398 home visits at government sites and 979 at NGO sites (Lijimol et al., IJPC 2020).
- **Services delivered:** "visiting bedridden patients at home with the help of ASHA workers … distribute waterbeds, wheelchairs, diapers and antiseptic lotions. Vehicles … hired on contract" (Onmanorama, Jul 2026).
- **PHC concessions:** "Exemption from obtaining out-patient tickets; Medicines dispensed for up to 6 weeks at a time for palliative care patients" (guideline table, PMC9983804).
- **Governance:** Palliative Care Management Committees (PMCs) at each LSG. The LSG chair heads it, the PHC Medical Officer is member secretary, and members include Kudumbashree, NGOs and the nurse (PMC9983804).
- **Volunteers:**
  - Asked to give "at least one hour a week" (CM, Jun 2025).
  - Registered via the "Sannadha Sena" portal [URL malformed in the UNI report; unverified].
  - "Volunteers … will be given ward-level training before being deployed" (ML, my translation, Asianet, 5 Mar 2025).
  - Policy goal: "a trained volunteer … near every bedridden patient" (ML, my translation, Asianet, 2 Mar 2025).
- **Training:**
  - Policy targets "500-1000 volunteers … three days of hands-on training in each district".
  - Courses: a 10-day Foundation Course; 6-week certificate courses in palliative medicine and palliative nursing; "six-day refresher training for all existing community nurses".
  - Palliative training centres now exist in every district (Asianet, Mar 2025).
  - Kerala now trains other states: Himachal sent "first batch of 30 professionals … 10-day programme in Thiruvananthapuram" [Tribune, search summary].
- **Opioids:**
  - Kerala adopted the model narcotic rules in 1998 (Lancet 2022).
  - The 2019 policy targets oral and injectable morphine on the KMSCL list and morphine "in all Primary Health Centres/Family Health Centres where a trained doctor is available".
  - Scroll: "only doctors with training in palliative care are permitted to give patients morphine."
- **Caregiver support:**
  - Carer allowance of ₹1,000/month (2026-27 budget).
  - Livelihood and rehabilitation schemes in the policy.
  - The Lancet Commission notes volunteers keep children in school and help bereaved spouses find work.
  - Families are taught practical care: "families were taught to use coconut water for hydration."
- **Telehealth:**
  - Malabar Cancer Centre has run e-palliative home care since 2013: the nurse brings "a laptop … the patient and their caregiver … communicate with their palliative care physician." Overall satisfaction was 4.39/5 (n=120; IJPC 2022; ehospice 2021).
  - Pallium India runs a telehealth helpline in Malayalam [search summary].
  - The grid is described as "using CARE to coordinate telehealth and community health workers" (COSCUP 2025 talk abstract, OHC).
- **Advance care planning / living wills:**
  - A Living Will counter opened at Kollam Government Medical College's palliative unit in Nov 2024 [The Week, search summary].
  - "no record on how many people have prepared 'Living Wills'" [search summary].
  - The Keralam Senior Citizens (Care and Protection of Rights) Bill, 2026 reportedly mandates more palliative, home-based and dementia care and "gives official recognition to advance medical directives" [Statesman, search summary only; page did not load].
- **Policy direction now (UDF, Oct 2026):** the minister wants palliative care "from the first stage when long-term illnesses are detected", linked to the "Hrudayaragam" home-screening pilot in Idukki, Nilambur and Vattiyoorkavu, "to be expanded to all 140 constituencies" (ML, my translation, Times Kerala, 27 Oct 2026).

## 3. Tech they use

**CARE (OHC).** OHC's page "CARE Home Care Grid" says: "Kerala's palliative-care grid is the strongest proof point: CARE supports a statewide care-coordination model across patients, home visits, care teams, voluntary organizations, public facilities, and program dashboards."
- Listed features: patient registry, "Mobile-first workflows for visit lists, vitals, symptoms, nursing needs, medicine needs, caregiver concerns, notes, and next visit dates", volunteer/NGO coordination, referral, dashboards.
- On OHC's AI roadmap: "Voice-native visit notes … in local languages"; "Follow-up drafts … caregiver instructions."
- **Everything is staff-side.** CARE has no family-entered data, which matches our CARE_VS_SAHARA doc.

**The grid as designed** (ML, my translation, Asianet, 2 and 5 Mar 2025):
- registering patients and ensuring follow-up care
- volunteer registration and training
- NGO registration
- "a facility for the public to choose the services they need"
- dashboards at "state, district, panchayat and ward levels"
- a public dashboard

The 2019 policy wanted an "omnichannel portal … ensuring … people who do not have access to the internet are included" and a "common online reporting system".

**health.kerala.care** is a different product. It is the "Vibe 4 Wellness" / Arogyam Anandam citizen portal: BMI, BP and sugar checkers, CBAC NCD risk screening, cancer symptom check, UHID registration, and a "Find JAK" locator. Footer: "Kerala Care is developed for the community by Govt. of Kerala in collaboration with the Open Healthcare Network." It states: "All calculations happen on your device, and your data never leaves your browser." **It has no palliative or family module.** It still shows the CM and HM portraits from the old government.

**Other tech:**
- Kerala State Digital Health Mission UHID, which links records across government facilities.
- eSanjeevani (on the NHM site).
- MCC's open-source e-Palliative web app (with C-DIT).

**Older practice:** paper registers and nurse diaries. The Mavoor study used "case records, nurses diary notes" (IJPC 2012).

**WhatsApp:** I found no public Kerala government WhatsApp or family app **[gap]**.

## 4. Pain points and gaps they describe publicly

- **Money and continuity:** Kannur LSG palliative care is at a "standstill". Nurse and driver salaries are "pending for the last six months"; "Fresh purchase of palliative care equipment and medicines is also not taking place." Cause: the Plan Fund instalment lapsed during the change of government (Onmanorama, 31 Jul 2026).
- **Uneven quality between LSGs:** "In some LGs, the programme was weak and mostly limited to the supply of medicines, basic aids and appliances" (PMC9983804, 2023). "Compliance with policy guidelines seems to be poor for both the LSGIs … lacks a public health approach to end-of-life care" (IJPC 2017).
- **Documentation and symptom reporting (directly relevant to Sahara):**
  - "Patient-reported pain was routinely documented by 21% (21/100) and 65% (65/100) of the GO and NGO sites."
  - "Psychosocial and spiritual issues were documented routinely in only 4% (4/100) and 48% (47/99)."
  - "Only 24% … had full-time physicians with training in palliative care" (Lijimol et al., IJPC 2020).
- **Doctor shortage:** "the state still has a shortage of palliative care doctors. Not all centres have a permanent doctor" (Scroll, 2024).
- **Opioid access:**
  - "only 57% … and 55% … reported 'always' or 'often' having an uninterrupted supply of oral morphine" (IJPC 2021 letter).
  - Kerala consumption is "only about one hundredth" of optimal, with "almost unbelievably high variation in opioid access between various districts" (Pallium, 2017).
  - The Lancet Commission (2017) tells of a patient who threatened suicide during a morphine stock-out.
- **Caregiver burden:**
  - "96% of patients had a care-giver at home, 85% of these care-givers being female" (BMC 2018).
  - Caregivers "have physically demanding, psychologically stressful and socially restrictive experiences" (BMC 2019).
  - Many "quit their jobs to perform caregiving" (Cureus 2025, PMC12085769).
- **Volunteers are both strength and risk:** volunteer involvement was seen "as a strength … whilst simultaneously being a challenge" (BMC 2019).
- **Rigidity:** "some consider this progress to be at the cost of innovation … health-care workers … deliver only what they are contracted to do" (Lancet 2022).
- **ACP and living wills:** there are no clear rules. Only one institution, Parippally GMC, has a permanent medical board [Onmanorama, Oct 2025, search summary].
- **Ageing:** 18.2% of Kerala is over 60, projected to reach 22.8% by 2036 (South First, Jan 2026). The UDF says the health system is "overstretched … weak coordination" (South First, Feb 2026).

## 5. Published research and data relevant to Sahara

- **Lijimol, Krishnan, Rajagopal, Gopal, Booth.** IJPC 2020, PMC7888431. Telephone survey of 200 providers covering documentation, morphine and staffing. Best evidence for a symptom-reporting gap.
- **Philip RR et al.**
  - BMC Palliative Care 2018: 20-year Malappuram cohort; diagnosis mix shifted to dementia 19%, stroke 17%, cancer 32%; visit frequency; caregiver profile.
  - BMC Palliative Care 2019: qualitative study, "Small small interventions, big big roles".
- **Kochuvilayil, Rajalakshmi, Krishnan, Vijayanand.** Public Health Action 2023. Study of the PMC committees.
- **Cureus 2025** (PMC12085769): built its own Caregiver Burden Scale for a secondary home-based palliative care unit.
- **MCC e-palliative satisfaction study** (IJPC 2022). Uses a validated Malayalam patient satisfaction questionnaire (e-Palliative PSQM).
- **Malayalam-validated tools that exist:** NCCN Distress Thermometer (IJPC vol 24) and FACIT-Sp-Ex (2024) [search summaries].
- **ESAS/IPOS in Malayalam:** I found no state-adopted ESAS/IPOS and no Malayalam validation **[gap/unverified]**.
- **Mavoor 2012 symptom profile:** weakness 41.3%, tiredness 31.7%, pain 27%.
- **Rajagopal, Karim, Booth.** J Global Oncology 2017. District-level morphine data.

## 6. How Sahara could help, what they would not want, and the first ask

**Fits, each feature matched to a stated gap:**
1. **Family symptom diary (0–10) shown to the nurse before each visit.** Answers pain documented at only 21% of government sites (Lijimol 2020) and doctor visits of about once a year (Philip 2018). It also fits OHC's own CARE field list, "symptoms … caregiver concerns".
2. **Medicine taken/missed log and stock-out flag.** Answers morphine supply that is uninterrupted at only 55–57% of sites. Families could report "running low" early to the PHC, which dispenses up to 6 weeks of medicine.
3. **Caregiver support and check-in.** Answers the female, job-quitting caregiver burden (BMC 2018/2019, Cureus 2025). It could also feed the Category A/B/C reassessment the policy requires "after each home visit".
4. **Consented goals-of-care record, reviewed by a doctor and confirmed by the family.** Answers the absent living-will records, and fits the 2026 Senior Citizens Bill's recognition of advance directives [pending verification]. Kerala's palliative tradition already includes "honest conversations about prognosis" (Lancet 2022).
5. **Family invites by WhatsApp link.** Would link the volunteer ("mediators between families and the programme", BMC 2019) to the family and the nurse.
6. **Fit with the current minister's push for earlier palliative care** and the Hrudayaragam home screening: ongoing family-side monitoring between visits.

**What they would likely not want:**
- **A parallel registry or a separate server holding state patient data.** The grid exists precisely to unify systems, and it passed a security audit. Sahara should feed CARE, for example as a CARE plugin or through its OTP patient side, not compete with it.
- **Hindi/English only.** Sahara currently has **no Malayalam**. This is a hard blocker for Kerala.
- **More work for nurses or the PMC.** Nurses are already unpaid in places and are the "custodian of all records".
- **Anything that looks commercial or political** during a change of government.
- **Opioid workflows outside the authorised doctor**: only trained palliative doctors handle morphine.

**Best first ask / pilot**
- **Who:** approach through OHC, which owns CARE and the grid deployment, together with the Aardram Palliative Care state nodal officer (Dr Mathews Numpeli) and NHM Kerala. Kerala Digital University is the grid lead.
- **What:** a 3-month pilot in one LSG (one PMC with its palliative nurse, about 30–50 Category A/B families).
- **How:** a Malayalam Sahara build. Family-entered symptom scores (ESAS-style) and missed-dose flags appear in the nurse's CARE visit list.
- **Success measures:**
  - share of visits with patient-reported pain documented (against the 21% baseline)
  - medicine-shortage alerts raised early
  - caregiver burden at the start and end
  - number of goals-of-care records confirmed by families
- **Data:** stays on the state/CARE infrastructure, with consent, under the DPDP Act.
- **Fallback:** a pilot with an NGO NNPC unit (IPM-linked) or with Malabar Cancer Centre's e-palliative home-care team.
