# Niram RIMH Palliative Care Centre, Tumkur: research report (read-only, 27 Oct 2026)

**Bottom line.** Niram is a new centre. It is funded by a family foundation and runs on staff with no published research yet. Home care started in October 2025, the inpatient ward in April 2026, and CARE onboarding in November 2025. It is already live on its own CARE instance, and Kannada is enabled there. Its own documents keep naming four family-side jobs: caregiver education, phone follow-up between visits, advance care planning / goals-of-care conversations, and a year of bereavement follow-up. Those match Sahara closely. Nothing public says what tools they use to measure symptoms. Their home-care logistics are run from a Google Sheet. A public GitHub repo for that work appears to contain a file with columns for patient name, address and phone (see §3; this needs private handling).

Raw downloads (pages, PDFs, CARE JS) are in (scratch path)

## 2. How they work day to day

- **Services** (centre page): inpatient care, outpatient visits, home-based support, rehab, psychosocial counselling, and "Telehealth & Remote Care: Phone- and video-based check-ins and follow-ups." Also 24/7 nursing, pharmacy, basic diagnostics, a family lounge and a grief-counselling space. The counsellor JD adds a laboratory.
- **Home-care flow** (Hans India / Tripura Star, 23–25 Jun 2026): "Once the social worker identifies and screens a patient, the home care team visits the family … In addition to visiting patients' homes, the team does regular tele follow-ups."
- **Visit frequency** (route-planner README, https://github.com/rupazb/niram_homecare_routeplanning): bedbound patients weekly, chairbound fortnightly, mobile monthly. Visits are clustered by geography across Tumkur district and start and end at the hospital. The author's link to Niram is **[unverified]**, but the README says "Niram Palliative Care Center's single ambulance".
- **Bereavement:** "bereavement support to families of deceased patients periodically with a plan to continue to be with them for at least a year." The counsellor goes on bereavement visits (counsellor JD, https://palliumindia.org/2026/09/counsellor-niram-palliative-care-center, 5 Sep 2026).
- **Morphine:** "Available (Under Registered Doctor's License)" (https://palliumindia.org/clinics/karnataka). The pharmacist JD asks for knowledge of "controlled substances" and to "Educate patients and caregivers on medication usage, storage, and disposal."
- **Caregiver support:** the nurse JD says "offering education and guidance on palliative care practices and self-care techniques." The social worker JD says "Facilitate discussions around end-of-life care preferences, advance directives" and "support group meetings for patients, caregivers, and bereaved families."
- **Volunteers:** recruited through a Google Form (not opened). They want medical students, retirees, artists and spiritual-care volunteers.
- **Training and research:** a JHU curriculum and research fellowship are planned, and a Research and Development Head was advertised on 21 Apr 2025.
  - A JHU Gupta-Klinsky India Institute "needs assessment study for Niram" ran 1–3 Jun 2026 with Aarohan Partners, as "first phase of a larger Human-Centered Design process". This comes from a search snippet only; the JHU pages were blocked by Cloudflare **[unverified text]**.
- **Language:** every JD requires Kannada ("Proficiency in Kannada is essential to effectively communicate with patients and families"; the Medical Director JD says "Fluency in Kannada is mandatory").

## 3. Tech they use

- **CARE HMIS** (OHC blog, https://ohc.network/blog/care-dashboards-testing-new-deployments, Nov 2025): "a 75-bed NGO-managed palliative care center with outpatient and home-visit services, began onboarding to CARE HMIS. The team set up a dedicated instance, configured facility structures, migrated patient data, imported stock lists, created custom questionnaires, configured user roles, and trained staff. Niram entered pilot phase with go-live expected after stabilization."
  - Niram is also named as a deployment in OHC blogs: care-core-as-operating-layer, plugs-v3-extensible-care-platform (May 2026), why-open-healthcare-needs-a-foundation.
- **Live instance:** https://care.nirampalliativecare.org returns 200, a login page I did not use. Its public build config, read from the static JS bundle, shows:
  - API at careapi.nirampalliativecare.org
  - Sentry environment "niram-palliative"
  - `REACT_ALLOWED_LOCALES:"en,hi,ta,ml,mr,kn"`, so Kannada is enabled
  - `REACT_ENABLE_QUESTIONNAIRE_DRAFT:"true"`
  - `REACT_ENABLED_APPS:""`, so no front-end plugins are configured at build time
  - Go-live status is **[unverified]**.
- **Google Sheet route planner:** a Google Sheet is "the single source of truth for patient data" for home-visit routing. It works with an Apps Script write-back and OpenRouteService routing. Fields are mobility, CA/NON CA, diagnosis, date of death, address and phone.
  - This means home-care logistics sit outside CARE **[inference]**.
- **Privacy risk (report it privately; do not publicise):** the public repo serves `patients_cleaned.csv` (46 rows) and `patients_all.geojson`. The CSV header is `REGISTRATION_NUMBER,NAME,GENDER,AGE,DATE_OF_ENROLLMENT,DIAGNOSIS,…,DATE_OF_DEATH,ADDRESS,PHONE_NUMBER,latitude,longitude`. I read only the header, not the rows, so whether the data is real or synthetic is **[unverified]**.
  - A careful heads-up to Niram (connect@) would be a good-faith first contact. I sent nothing.
- **Other tools:** Google Forms (volunteer, RSVP), Pallium India telehealth support. No WhatsApp helpline, patient app or family app is mentioned anywhere.
- **Stated digital needs:**
  - Medical Director JD: "Implement feedback mechanisms to address patient and family needs … leveraging data and analytics."
  - Counsellor JD: "Develop and maintain appropriate psychosocial assessment formats and documentation tools for inpatient, outpatient and home-care settings … integrated appropriately into the patient's clinical record."
- **Social media:** none found (no LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook or YouTube). The Development Associate JD mentions "keeping the website and any social medial platforms updated".

## 4. Pain points and gaps they describe publicly

- **Rural reach and staff shortage:** blog-why-palliative-care-matters.html says "fewer than 1,000 trained palliative care specialists … Most services are urban-centered, creating a rural access gap." Shetty and Prabhakar in The Hindu (3 Jul 2025, https://www.thehindu.com/opinion/op-ed/integrating-compassion-prioritising-palliative-care/article69765221.ece) write "One of the primary barriers is the shortage of trained professionals" and propose "task-shifting", "public awareness … late-stage access", and Ayushman Bharat coverage.
- **Burden on families:** the blog lists "Greater financial burden on families, High emotional stress, Frequent hospitalizations, many of which can be avoided."
- **What families go through** (counsellor JD): "fear and anxiety, loss of function … anticipatory grief … communication or decision-making challenges"; "discharge planning where psychosocial considerations may affect continuity of care."
- **Local context** (IDR series funded by AIF; not Niram's own words):
  - Bangalore Baptist Hospital, which also works in Tumakuru (https://idronline.org/article/health/strengthening-access-to-palliative-care-in-rural-areas/): "it fell on the families to provide care, who mostly did not have any information or resources to guide them"; "absence of formal medical records"; "Care providers often hesitate to prescribe morphine"; "We also train family members to administer medications."
  - The gram panchayat article: "caregivers will lose a day's wage" travelling to appointments.
- **Gaps that are only inferred:** with weekly to monthly visit tiers, symptom changes between visits are caught only by staff-initiated tele follow-ups **[inference]**. Advance care planning is a named staff skill, but no ACP form or tool is described.

## 5. Published research and data

- No papers by Niram. PubMed searches for "niram palliative" and "tumakuru palliative" returned 0. "tumkur palliative" returned one unrelated case report. Devasahayam M and Prabhakar A searches returned 0.
- No symptom instrument (ESAS, IPOS, POS) is named publicly. CARE "custom questionnaires" exist, but their content is unknown.

## 6. How Sahara could help

| Their pain point (source) | Sahara feature |
|---|---|
| "regular tele follow-ups" between weekly to monthly visits (Hans India; README tiers) | Family symptom diary (0–10) and 3D pain body map, so the tele follow-up starts from data |
| Caregivers need education on "medication usage, storage, and disposal"; morphine is prescribed (pharmacist JD; Pallium directory) | Medicines list with a taken/missed log, so families can show morphine adherence and breakthrough doses |
| Advance care planning and "difficult conversations around goals of care" are core skills of the social workers and doctors (centre page; social worker JD) | Goals-of-care conversation recorded with consent, then a doctor-reviewed note, then the family confirms the exact version |
| "documentation tools for … home-care settings … integrated into the clinical record" (counsellor JD) | Structured family-reported data that could be mapped into their CARE questionnaires later. CARE has no family side today |
| One-year bereavement follow-up (Hans India) | Family accounts that continue after the patient dies; visits and documents |
| Kannada mandatory | **Gap:** Sahara is Hindi/English only, and their CARE has `kn` enabled. Kannada is a precondition for any pilot |
| Ambulance visit days from a Google Sheet | Visit schedule visible to families, so they know the next visit day |

**What they would likely NOT want:**
- Another staff-side system or duplicate data entry alongside CARE, which they only just migrated to.
- Cloud storage of identifiable data without clear consent and data-protection terms; they handle opioids and work with JHU and ethics processes.
- Hindi-first screens.
- Anything that replaces Pallium telehealth or their counsellor's role.

**Best first ask / pilot:**
1. Approach Dr Bhanushree or Abhirami P C, the ACP and social-work leads, through connect@nirampalliativecare.org. The privacy heads-up could also go through this channel, framed carefully.
2. Propose a small Kannada pilot with home-care families: about 10–15 bedbound or chairbound patients on the weekly tier. Families log symptoms and medicines between visits, and staff use the data on tele follow-up calls.
3. Make it a research pilot with their R&D/JHU arm (outcomes: unplanned calls/admissions, caregiver burden). It could fit the JHU human-centred design phase.
4. Do not ask for CARE integration up front.
