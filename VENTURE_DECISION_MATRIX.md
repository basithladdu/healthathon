# Health-a-thon 2026: final problem-statement and venture decision

Research cut-off: 25 August 2026

## Decision

Keep the registered problem statement:

**Cancer Care -> Clinician focused -> Patient Follow-up & Continuity of Care**

Build and pitch:

**Continuity Loop — a clinician-controlled goals-of-care follow-up and verified clinical-handoff workflow.**

The visible wedge is the moment an unfamiliar emergency physician retrieves the latest physician-verified goals-of-care summary. The operational product behind that moment identifies unfinished conversations, assigns an owner and review date, guides the next clinician conversation, preserves source-linked versions and closes the follow-up loop.

The longer-term company should not be described as a standalone “goals-of-care PDF app.” It should be an **oncology continuity and clinical-handoff operating layer**, with goals of care as the first high-trust module.

Confidence: **high** that this is the best choice for this team; **moderate** that it is the best standalone venture across all 13 statements because buyer and pilot evidence remain incomplete.

## Why this remains the best team-adjusted choice

The original selection was made quickly, but subsequent evidence supports it for reasons that were not known at registration:

1. Two clinicians proposed and explained a specific failure from real practice.
2. They can author the conversation framework and recruit clinicians for approximately 20 simulated cases.
3. The problem is longitudinal: discussions are unfinished, undocumented, revisited and lost across transitions of care.
4. The prototype can demonstrate a complete closed loop without a live EMR, Aadhaar, ABHA or real patient data.
5. The narrow goals-of-care handoff makes the entry memorable even if generic follow-up is crowded.
6. The same infrastructure can expand into other oncology continuity modules after the hackathon.

The strongest alternative on market economics alone is Financial & Administrative Support, but that space demands changing scheme rules, claims standards, institutional integrations and incumbent relationships that this team does not currently possess. The strongest semantic match to the original document is Consultation Readiness, but switching categories would sacrifice the registered position without improving the actual build or clinical access.

## Prize-pool arithmetic

Verified programme facts:

- The advertised pool is **₹54+ lakh in total value**, including cash, technology credits and partner benefits.
- It is split evenly across Cancer Care, Diabetes Care and Maternal & Child Health: approximately **₹18 lakh of advertised value per clinical track**.
- The official guide says 30 teams reach the finale and every finalist receives an award.
- No official payout by rank, problem statement or cash-versus-credit component is published.

What cannot be concluded:

- The 13 problem statements do not each receive “a few lakhs.” The pool is divided by three clinical tracks, not by 13 use cases.
- Thirty finalists do not imply 30 equal payments.
- ₹54 lakh / 30 = **₹1.8 lakh average advertised value per finalist** only under an equal-allocation assumption. It is not a promised payment and some value may be credits or benefits.
- If each clinical track happened to have ten finalists and its ₹18 lakh were divided equally, the same ₹1.8 lakh average follows. Neither the ten-team split nor equal awards is published.

The user's “30 shortlisted teams” should therefore be used only as the expected finalist denominator. On the official August timeline, the Top 30 are announced later; it is not evidence that 30 teams have already been selected as of 25 August.

There is also a programme-source conflict to clarify: the Reskilll guide describes a Top 30 finale, while a Koita Foundation programme page has described a Top 120 build sprint followed by a Top 10 grand jury. The team should ask the organiser which structure and payout table govern this edition.

## Product and company thesis

### Hackathon product

1. Treating clinician opens a guided conversation.
2. Checklist records what was discussed, not discussed or deferred.
3. A source note is converted into structured fields with direct provenance.
4. Physician reviews every field; patient/surrogate acknowledgement is recorded.
5. A new version is released without overwriting the prior one.
6. An unfamiliar receiving physician records an access purpose and retrieves the latest verified summary.
7. The continuity team owns due reviews, outreach and unresolved questions.

### 60–90 day pilot hypothesis

Run 20 synthetic or fully anonymised simulated conversations across varied speakers, languages and clinical scenarios. Compare the product-generated record against a clinician-authored reference.

Measure:

- required-domain coverage;
- unsupported statement rate;
- unresolved-item capture;
- clinician correction rate;
- time to prepare and retrieve the verified handoff;
- agreement on latest-version identification;
- user-rated clarity for receiving clinicians.

Do not claim patient outcomes, diagnostic accuracy, treatment effects or legal validity from this pilot.

### Commercial expansion

Start with one cancer centre and one workflow owner. Sell an institution-controlled continuity layer, not a consumer chatbot. Expand only after evidence into:

- follow-up recovery and navigator worklists;
- treatment-transition handoffs;
- survivorship and palliative-care continuity;
- caregiver-authorised task coordination;
- source-linked consultation preparation;
- standards-based export or hospital integration.

Potential buyers are oncology programme heads, palliative-care services, hospital digital-transformation teams and quality/operations leaders. A buyer has not yet been confirmed.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Clinical note, not living will or Advance Medical Directive.
- No automatic prognosis, eligibility, urgency or treatment recommendation.
- No claim of legal force.
- No autonomous patient/family edits; revisions occur through a verified clinician workflow.
- No real patient data in the hackathon prototype.
- No live AI, ABDM, identity, cryptographic or hospital integration claim unless actually implemented and tested.
- Voice capture remains optional research until multilingual, multi-speaker and one-hour-session performance is measured.

## Remaining decisions for the clinicians

1. Approve the exact conversation domains and wording.
2. Name the first real workflow owner and pilot setting.
3. Define when a review is due without algorithmic clinical scoring.
4. Decide the minimum acknowledgement process.
5. Define which receiving clinicians can retrieve a record and under what access purposes.
6. Produce the 20 simulation scenarios and clinician-authored reference records.
7. Select one primary operational KPI for the submission.

Until those are answered, the product is a strong validated prototype direction—not a deployable clinical system.
