
Sahaara – Clinical Specification
Clinical companion to the tech spec. It states the clinical content, logic and safeguards the build must encode, cross-referenced to tech-spec rule IDs (L1–L10 legal, V1–V9 validation) and to Sahaara v1.0 section numbers. A bare § means Sahaara; "this doc §" means a section here. Items marked [Sign-off] need the medical lead's decision before build freeze.
1. Scope, population and setting
Eligible patients (Sahaara §2.1): adults with advanced, life-limiting illness where the surprise question ("Would I be surprised if this patient died in the next 12 months?") is answered no, plus at least one of:
No remaining disease-modifying options, or options declined.
Progressive decline: poor performance status, largely bed-bound, minimal oral intake, withdrawn.
Repeated unplanned admissions for the same problem.
Patient or family has asked about end of life or avoiding hospital.
When to complete (Appendix D.2): OPD visit when disease-modifying options end; discharge after an unplanned admission; ICU step-down; enrolment in home palliative care. Not for the first time in the resuscitation bay — that records panic, not preference. The app should not offer "new ECTPR" from an ED encounter without a warning.
Who completes: a clinician who has completed observed training (Appendix D.1); the treating consultant signs (V7). Palliative clinician and nurse co-sign where involved. [Sign-off] Can a palliative-care consultant who is not the primary oncologist sign as "treating consultant"?
Out of scope: paediatrics (<18 y), mental-health advance directives (Mental Healthcare Act 2017), and any act intended to end life.

3. Clinical definition of each decision field
These definitions fix what each stored value means clinically; the app prints them as written. Field names match tech spec §5.
Field
Values
Clinical meaning and interpretation
goal_of_care
A Prioritise life · B Balanced / ceiling of care · C Prioritise comfort
A: all medically effective treatment incl. ICU and ventilation. B: treat reversible problems within the ceilings recorded, as time-limited trials. C: symptom control is the goal; transfer only if comfort cannot be achieved where the patient is.
hospital_transfer
Yes, any significant deterioration · Only for listed reasons · No
Listed reasons (§7): uncontrolled pain; uncontrolled breathlessness; significant bleeding; seizures; fall with suspected fracture; blocked/displaced catheter, tube or stent; suspected infection needing IV treatment; acute confusion/agitation not settling; caregiver unable to cope (respite). "No" = care where they are; call the listed numbers before an ambulance.
Cpr
Attempt · DNACPR
Applies only to cardiopulmonary arrest. Says nothing about any other treatment. DNACPR reason: clinical futility / patient refusal / burden outweighs benefit; record who it was discussed with.
resp_ceiling
Level 0–5
0 comfort measures only (opioid, anxiolytic, fan, positioning, suction; O₂ only if it relieves); 1 nasal prongs/simple mask (record max L/min); 2 non-rebreather/venturi; 3 HFNC; 4 NIV (CPAP/BiPAP); 5 intubation + invasive ventilation. The ticked level is the highest acceptable; all lower levels remain available; nothing above it starts without a fresh discussion with the surrogate and treating consultant.
Icu
Yes · Time-limited trial (days) · No
"No" = ward-level or comfort care only. A trial needs duration, formal review date/time and named reviewers (§10.1).

Feeding
Comfort oral feeding · NG: no / ≤ n days then review / yes · PEG: no / only after palliative-care discussion / yes
Clinically assisted nutrition is medical treatment (Harish Rana, 2026). Declining to start a tube is withholding; removing an existing tube is withdrawal and follows the board pathway (this doc §7). Mouth care and hand feeding always continue.
Hydration
Oral sips only · Limited · Full maintenance
Limited = ≤ 500–1000 mL normal saline/24 h, IV or SC, matched to urine output, reviewed daily (NCG). Reduce or stop if oedema, wet secretions, distension or worsening breathlessness.
Unanswered field = no preference recorded = standard care (V1). This must never be read as refusal.

5. Capacity, disclosure and surrogate decision-making
Capacity (Saanthvana §4.1) is decision-specific and assessed at the time, not taken from old notes. Record the basis against the four abilities: understand, retain, weigh, communicate. A patient with capacity can change or cancel any part of the record verbally at any time, and their current wish overrides the document (§18.1). Fluctuating capacity requires re-assessment before acting on any section (C7).
Disclosure (§5.3): withholding prognosis at family request is common in Indian practice and is not itself a reason to refuse completing the record. A patient with capacity who asks directly must be answered honestly; the handling plan is recorded (C8).

"Family consensus" is recorded as a note, not as a basis of authority. The order sets no ranking among relatives; disagreement follows §18.2 — one family meeting with surrogate, dissenters, consultant and palliative care; return to what the patient would have wanted; if unresolved, the institutional ethics committee, with current treatment continued meanwhile.
Surrogate standard: substituted judgement first (what the patient would have chosen); best interests with the team where that is unknown (§4.3 declaration).
Non-beneficial treatment (§18.3): the team is not obliged to provide it; explain why, offer a second opinion and transfer, and document.
Financial coercion: in self-pay families a limit may reflect money, not values. Ask directly and offer social-work and scheme-eligibility review before finalising (Appendix D.4). The Supreme Court in Harish Rana flagged the same risk.
6. Emergency use — ED and ambulance
ED Quick View content, in this order: hospital transfer · CPR · respiratory ceiling · ICU · goal of care · allergies · implanted device · named decision-maker (24 h phone) · treating consultant and palliative contact · capacity flag · AMD status · version, signed date, review-due date.
"If in doubt, resuscitate" (Sahaara §1) — full standard emergency care when:
the record is unsigned, out of date or cannot be found;
the deterioration is new, reversible and unrelated to the underlying illness — injury, choking, drug reaction, hypoglycaemia;
the patient has capacity and says something different now;
the record is ambiguous.
Clarify with the treating team afterwards.
Clarification for the tech team: the tech spec shows an out-of-date record with an "OUT OF DATE" banner. Clinically, condition 1 means an out-of-date record is information, not instruction: the banner must say standard care applies. [Sign-off] Show the content beneath that banner, or hide it?
Ambulance crews (§7, §7.1) resuscitate by default; the record has effect only if a signed copy is physically handed over. Options recorded: full pre-hospital resuscitation and transport · transport with symptom relief only (O₂ for breathlessness, analgesia, positioning; no CPR, no intubation) · transport home for last days with nurse escort. The wallet card (Appendix E) carries: patient, UHID, diagnosis, CPR, ceiling, transfer, number to call first, surrogate, where the full record is held, signing doctor and date, and the line "if unclear or the problem is new and reversible — treat fully and call".
Recognising the dying phase (Appendix B) triggers urgent review of the record: falling BP, cold mottled peripheries, jaw breathing, Cheyne–Stokes respiration, reduced urine output. Where signs are ambiguous, take a second opinion — a wrongly declared dying phase is self-fulfilling.
Mandatory review triggers (§18.4), which reset review_due: any new admission · transfer between settings · significant change in condition either way · request by patient or family · start of a new line of treatment · routine interval [Sign-off: weeks].
7. Withholding vs withdrawing — the board pathway (tech L8)
Most of the ECTPR is about withholding (not starting) treatment, which the plan itself directs. Withdrawing treatment already running — ventilation, pressors, dialysis, or an existing feeding tube (C6) — follows the Supreme Court procedure below. The app records the wish and may display this pathway; it never triggers withdrawal.

withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment · SC 2018 as modified 2023
The same boards apply without an AMD; consent then comes from next of kin, next friend or guardian. In Harish Rana (11 Mar 2026) the Court applied this process, held that clinically assisted nutrition is medical treatment, required withdrawal to be a planned palliative process rather than abandonment, and directed that JMFCs receive hospital intimations and that district CMOs keep panels for Secondary Boards. Tell families plainly: agreeing now not to start a ventilator is a smaller step than stopping one later (Sahaara Appendix C.1).
