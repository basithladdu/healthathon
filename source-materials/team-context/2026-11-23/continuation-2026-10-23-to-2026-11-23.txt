[23/11/26, 6:49:23 PM] ~ SVK: Designing for caregivers of   caregiving prompts 


1. Passive Behavioral Signals 
 
 * Sudden Engagement Drop-Off: If a caregiver who logs medications, symptoms, or appointments daily suddenly stops opening the app for 3–5 consecutive days, trigger a "quiet mode" 

 * Abrupt Cessation of Specific Tasks: If medication tracking stops entirely while the user still opens the app to view notes or messages, this is a strong indicator that active caregiving has paused or ended.

 * Integration Signals (I think we can pitch this as a later thing to do while working with hospitals or palliative care networks): If the app syncs with a patient portal, hospice care team, or shared family calendar, look for status changes (e.g., discharge to hospice, cancellation of recurring specialist appointments) to adjust the app's tone proactively

2. Gentle Check-In Copy & Framing
 low-friction options.
Instead of asking "How is [Patient Name] doing today?", use a soft status check after a period of inactivity:
> "We haven't checked in for a few days, and we're thinking of you. Has anything changed in your caregiving routine?"
>  * Things are steady, just taking a break from the app
>  * Our care needs or routine have shifted
>  * I'm no longer actively caregiving
>  * Remind me later
> 
 Selecting "I'm no longer actively caregiving" is accurate, dignified, and emotionally protective. It covers bereavement, transition to a full-time facility, or passing caregiving duties to someone else, without forcing the word "death" onto the screen before the user is ready.

3. Progressive Disclosure

If the user selects "I'm no longer actively caregiving" or "Our care needs have shifted", transition gently into a compassionate follow-up screen that lets them define what happened—or skip defining it altogether.
> "Thank you for letting us know. Caregiving transitions can carry a lot of weight, and we want to make sure this space supports you properly. Would you like to share what changed so we can adjust your experience?"
>  * [Patient Name] has passed away
>  * [Patient Name] moved to a care facility / hospice
>  * Someone else has taken over care
>  * I'd prefer not to say right now
> 
4. Designing the "After" Experience

 Notification Freeze: The millisecond they indicate they are no longer caregiving—or that the patient has passed—immediately cancel all queued reminders, medication alerts, and upbeat "streak" notifications.
 * Preserve, Don't Delete: Never wipe the patient’s profile or logs automatically. Caregivers often find comfort in looking back at notes, photos, or milestones. Archive the caregiving dashboard into a quiet "Memory / Archive" tab that they can access only when they choose to.
 * Pivot to Grief & Recovery Support: Offer an optional toggle to shift the app's focus from caregiving logistics to caregiver recovery and bereavement resources—or give them a one-tap option to pause their account for 30, 60, or 90 days without losing their data.

 * Empower Secondary Contacts: If the app allows family members or co-caregivers to share a care circle, allow a secondary member to update the status quietly so the primary caregiver doesn't have to bear the burden of administrative updates while grieving.
